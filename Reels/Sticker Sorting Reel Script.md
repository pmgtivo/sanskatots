# Sticker Sorting Reel Script

---

## Script

**(0:00 - 0:04):**
3 days. I spent 3 full days just sorting stickers.
And honestly, I am completely exhausted. But I am proud that it is finally done. Let me tell you how it went.

**(0:04 - 0:10):**
So our stickers finally arrived from the printing press.
I opened the box and I thought — okay, this is a one-hour job. Easy. Let me just sort them quickly and move on.

**(0:10 - 0:18):**
But here's the thing nobody tells you about Velcro activity books.
Each book has multiple activities. And each activity has its own set of Velcro stickers.
If those stickers are mixed up — even slightly — the worker who is assembling the book has no idea which sticker belongs to which activity.
The whole assembly line breaks.

**(0:18 - 0:26):**
So every single sticker has to be sorted — activity by activity, book by book — before we even touch the assembly.
That is not a one-hour job. That is not a one-day job.
That is apparently a three-day job.

**(0:26 - 0:33):**
And usually I have people to help me with this.
But my workers are all on summer vacation leave right now.
So it was just me. Alone. With thousands of tiny stickers and way too much determination.

**(0:33 - 0:42):**
Day one felt fine. Day two felt okay.
By day three, I was sitting on the floor, surrounded by sticker piles, asking myself some very serious life questions.
But I kept going. Because the sorting is not just organizing — it is the foundation for every single book we assemble.
If I do it right once, everything after this goes smoothly.

**(0:42 - 0:50):**
And now it is done. Every sticker is sorted. Every activity pile is ready.
This is what building a brand from scratch actually looks like — not always glamorous, but it matters.

**(0:50 - 1:00):**
We are launching our activity books in June, and every tiny thing you see us do is going into making them perfect for your child.
If you want to follow this journey, hit follow. The next update is coming soon.

## Visuals

1. **(0:00 - 0:04)** — Close-up of hands sorting a massive pile of stickers on a table. Tired, slightly disheveled look. Direct eye contact with the camera. No smile yet — let the fatigue be visible.

2. **(0:04 - 0:10)** — Shot of the box arriving / being opened. Cut to a wide shot showing the full spread of sticker sheets on the table, looking like a lot more work than expected.

3. **(0:10 - 0:18)** — Quick B-roll of a Velcro busy book being opened, showing different activity pages. Then cut to mixed-up sticker pile to visually show the problem.

4. **(0:18 - 0:26)** — Time-lapse or fast-cut montage of sorting in progress — hands picking, grouping, placing in small piles by activity.

5. **(0:26 - 0:33)** — Empty workspace in the background, just Nagarathna working alone. Maybe gesture around to show no one else is there. Slightly amused expression — laughing at yourself a little.

6. **(0:33 - 0:42)** — Day 1/2/3 text overlay. Cut to sitting-on-floor shot surrounded by piles, looking exhausted but still working. Can be candid or reenacted.

7. **(0:42 - 0:50)** — Beautiful shot of all sorted sticker piles, neatly arranged by activity. Satisfying visual. Proud but tired smile.

8. **(0:50 - 1:00)** — Hold one finished sorted book component up to camera. End with a warm smile and direct CTA to follow.

---

## Notes
- Keep the tone real and slightly self-deprecating — the exhaustion is part of the charm.
- Do not over-polish. The authenticity of "I did this alone for 3 days" is the hook.
- The sorting explanation (0:10 - 0:18) is the educational beat — keep it visual with the book, not just talking head.
