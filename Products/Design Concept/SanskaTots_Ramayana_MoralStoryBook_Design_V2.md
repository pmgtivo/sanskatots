## DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

### Core Principle: Every Page Is a Mini Comic
Each of the 22 story pages works like a **4–6 panel comic strip**. No full-page illustrations. Instead, small sequential panels tell the story like a cartoon strip — exactly how kids understand stories best.

### V2 Story Principle
The panels tell the **actual Ramayana story** — real characters, real events, real narrative sequence. The **Modern Life Moral** at the bottom of each page is the ONLY modernised element — one simple line connecting the ancient lesson to a child's life today. Parents can read the panels as a story and then talk about the moral together.

### Target Audience
- **Primary (Kids 2–5):** Cannot read. Panels guide the eye left → right, top → bottom. Big expressive faces, clear actions, bright colors.
- **Secondary (Parents):** Want to teach their child the actual Ramayana — with a clear modern moral they can discuss. Every page gives both.

### Design Rules

| Rule | Implementation |
|---|---|
| **80% Illustration / 20% Text** | Each panel has max 1 short caption (5–8 words). Title + Moral box below panels. |
| **Comic Panel Grid** | 4 panels = 2×2 grid. 5 panels = 2+3 or 3+2. 6 panels = 2×3 or 3×2 grid. |
| **Big Faces, Big Emotions** | All characters drawn in **chibi/kawaii** style — oversized heads, huge eyes, exaggerated expressions |
| **No Scary Imagery** | Ravana = grumpy/silly villain. Battles = cartoon action. No blood, no fear. |
| **Warm Indian Palette** | Saffron, golden yellow, sky blue, forest green, lotus pink — zero black or grey |
| **Consistent Characters** | Same chibi style for all characters across all 22 pages |
| **Modern Life Moral** | Every story ends with how the lesson connects to a child's **real daily life today** |
| **Speech Bubbles** | Key character lines are shown in round speech bubbles inside panels |
| **Panel Numbering** | Subtle ① ② ③ numbers in corner of each panel to guide reading order |

### PAGE 1 — "Good Morning, Guruji!" 🙏
**Ramayana Event: Young Rama Goes with Sage Vishwamitra to Protect the Yagna | Kanda: Bala Kanda | Theme: Respect for Teachers & Courage**

**Panel Grid: 6 panels (2×3 Grid)**

---

#### 📖 Story Context for Illustrator & Narrator
> Sage Vishwamitra — once a mighty king who gave up his throne to become the greatest sage of his time — had been performing a sacred yagna (fire ritual) deep in the forest to bring peace and blessings to the world. But two powerful demons, **Maricha** and **Subahu**, sent by the demon king Ravana, kept attacking the ritual — throwing bones and blood into the holy fire, destroying months of Vishwamitra's hard work. No ordinary army could stop them because the demons had magical powers that made them vanish and reappear. Vishwamitra knew that only **young Prince Rama** — who had a pure heart AND divine strength — could defeat these demons. So he walked all the way to Ayodhya to ask King Dasharatha for his beloved eldest son.

## PANEL ILLUSTRATION PRODUCTION NOTES

### For the Illustrator

1. **Consistency First:** Create a **master character sheet** before drawing a single panel. All 16 characters in the V2 character table must look identical across all 22 pages.
2. **Story Fidelity:** V2 panels show the ACTUAL Ramayana events — Kaikeyi's boons, the Lakshmana Rekha, Jatayu's attack, the Dronagiri mountain. Characters must be visually identifiable from their names.
3. **Panel Emotions:** Each panel tells ONE emotion clearly. Don't crowd panels with multiple emotions.
4. **Reading Flow:** Panels must be numbered ① ② ③ visually and designed for left to right, top to bottom reading.
5. **Speech Bubbles:** Round bubbles for speech. Cloud-shape for thoughts. Keep max 6 words per bubble.
6. **Action Lines:** Use comic-style speed lines behind characters for dynamic action moments.
7. **Expressions Library:** For each character, create an expression reference sheet — Happy, Sad, Angry, Scared, Brave, Proud, Surprised. Use consistently.
8. **Background Complexity:** Simple in panels — one clear background element (forest, palace, river). No busy backgrounds that compete with characters.
9. **Moral Box Design:** Every page — same lotus-border box at bottom. Saffron background, white bold text. Like a stamp the child can always find.
10. **V2 Special Notes:**
    - Page 3: Manthara must look sly and scheming — NOT overtly evil. She looks like a scheming maid.
    - Page 8: Jatayu must look old but noble — greyed feathers but blazing eyes.
    - Page 15: The squirrel's THREE GOLDEN STRIPES (from Rama's fingers) must be clearly visible in Panel 5.
    - Page 7: Rama's wooden sandals on the golden throne are a KEY image — contrast of simple vs grand.
    - Page 22: Panel 4 is the most technically complex — all 21 story memories as tiny glowing vignettes inside Hanuman's heart.
