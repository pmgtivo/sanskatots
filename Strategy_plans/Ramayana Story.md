# Ramayana Story

### 1. The Divine Princes of Ayodhya
Once upon a time, in a big golden kingdom called Ayodhya, a king Dasharatha had four wonderful sons. The eldest was Rama — kind, brave, and loved by everyone. The whole kingdom smiled when he walked by!

---

### 2. Rama Wins a Princess!
King Janaka is holding a contest whoever can string the massive, divine bow of Shiva will marry Princess Sita. Many tried, but failed. Rama walked up, picked it up… and He broke it! Sita smiled — Rama and Sita soon married.

---

### 3. Into the Forest: The Banishment
Because of an old promise King Dasharatha made to Queen Kaikeyi, Rama is banned from Ayodhya for 14 years. He accepted in calm. His wife Sita and loyal brother Lakshmana refuse to let him go alone. They walked bravely together into the dense forest.

---

### 4. The Golden Deer and the Trap
The demon king Ravana sees Sita in the forest and wants to kidnap her. He orders his demon uncle to transform into a beautiful, magical golden deer to lure Rama away. Sita, enchanting by the sparkling creature, begs Rama to catch it for her.

---

### 5. Sita is Captured by Ravana
While Rama chased the deer, the mean demon king Ravana came and took Sita away in his flying chariot, the Pushpaka Vimana. Sita cried for help. A brave vulture king, **Jatayu** tried to stop him — but Ravana was too strong.

---

### 6. Hanuman's Magnificent Leap
While searching for Sita, Rama meets Hanuman, the powerful monkey warrior. Hanuman offers his help. After learning that Sita is in Lanka, Hanuman uses his divine power to grow to a giant size and takes a monumental leap towards the island.

---

### 7. Building the Bridge to Lanka
Hanuman finds Sita and returns to Rama. To cross the vast ocean with the monkey army, Rama prays to the Sea God. A path is promised: the rocks will float if they are inscribed with Rama's name.

---

### 8. Victory: Light Over Darkness
The big battle began! Rama fought the scary ten-headed demon king Ravana. Rama defeats Ravana using his divine Brahmastra arrow, Sita was free! Everyone cheered and danced! Rama, Sita, and Lakshmana came home. Every house lit a little lamp — **diya** — to say *"Welcome home, Rama!"
