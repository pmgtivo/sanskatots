# 1. HOW SHARK TANK INDIA ACTUALLY WORKS

## 1.1 The Application Funnel

| Stage | Volume | Conversion | Timeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Online Application Submitted | ~1,00,000+ per season | 100% | Form opens ~6 months pre-shoot |
| Shortlisted (Round 1 — Phone Screen) | ~5,000–7,000 | 5–7% | 2–4 weeks after application |
| Audition Round (Video Pitch + Document Submission) | ~1,500 | ~25% of shortlist | 4–8 weeks |
| Mumbai Pitch Round (In-Person Mock Pitch) | ~400–500 | ~30% | Weeks before shoot |
| **On-Air Pitches (Filmed)** | **~150–200** | **~35–40%** | Shoot window (4–6 weeks) |
| **Aired On TV** | **~120–150** | ~75% of shot pitches | Cut decisions during edit |
| Got a Deal On Air | ~70–90 | ~55–60% of aired | — |
| **Deal Actually Closed Post Due-Diligence** | **~30–45** | **~45–55% of TV deals fall through** | 3–9 months after airing |

> **Shocking truth:** Roughly **half of all "deals" announced on Shark Tank India never close.** Due diligence kills them. This shapes your strategy: get the on-air announcement (massive PR value), then negotiate hard during DD.

## 1.2 Application — What You Submit

The official application (sonyliv.com/shark-tank-india-apply) requires:

- **Founder details** (name, age, education, background, contact)
- **Business basics** (name, category, year started, location)
- **Financials** (revenue last FY, projected, profit/loss, current burn)
- **Funding history** (bootstrapped? prior investors? loans?)
- **The "Ask"** (amount + equity %)
- **Why this business?** (3–5 line founder story)
- **Video pitch** (60–90 seconds, vertical, your face + product, NO professional editing — they want raw passion)
- **Photos of product / packaging / customers**

**Application opens for Season 6 typically in May–July 2026, shoots Sept–Nov 2026, airs Jan–Apr 2027.** Plan backwards from this.

## 1.3 Shoot Day — What Actually Happens

- **Pre-shoot prep (1–2 days in Mumbai):** wardrobe check, mock pitch with content team, set walkthrough, legal disclosures, NDA signing.
- **Shoot duration per pitch: 60–90 minutes** (TV edit shows only 8–15 minutes).
- **Sharks have NOT seen your deck or financials** — they hear you cold. The first 90 seconds = make or break.
- **Multiple takes are common** for entry walk and tagline delivery. Numbers and answers cannot be redone — those are real.
- **You CAN walk out without a deal.** You CAN reject all offers. You CAN come back next season.
- **The "All Sharks Out" board:** Anupam Mittal coined the phrase — getting all sharks out is statistically the most common outcome (~40–50% of pitches).

## 1.4 Post-Shoot — The Due Diligence Period

If you accept a deal on air:
1. **Term sheet within 30 days** (sharks' team drafts, you sign).
2. **Financial DD (45–90 days):** every invoice, GST return, bank statement, vendor PO scrutinized.
3. **Legal DD:** trademarks, IP ownership, contracts, employee agreements.
4. **Operational DD:** customer calls, supplier interviews, warehouse visits.
5. **SHA (Shareholders Agreement)** drafted — this is where deals die. Sharks add board seats, founder vesting, anti-dilution, ROFR clauses founders didn't expect.
6. **Money disbursed only after Pvt Ltd conversion + clean DD + signed SHA.**

**Median time from shoot → money in account: 4–8 months.** Plan your runway accordingly.

# 3. WHAT SHARKS UNIVERSALLY LOOK FOR IN A BUSINESS

The sharks evaluate you across **8 dimensions**, almost always in this priority order:

## 3.1 Founder & Founder–Market Fit (Weight: 30%)

The **single largest factor.** Sharks back people, not ideas.

What they assess:
- **Why YOU and not someone else?** Your unfair life-advantage in this market.
- **Domain credibility** — years in the category, products designed, customers spoken to.
- **Storytelling ability** — can you make them feel something in 60 seconds?
- **Coachability** — will you take feedback or argue defensively?
- **Resilience indicators** — did you bootstrap, sacrifice, persevere?
- **Founder–team completeness** — solo founder is a yellow flag; complementary co-founders are a green flag.

**SanskaTots gap to close:** You are a solo founder (sole proprietor). Sharks WILL ask: *"Who runs operations? Who handles tech? What if you fall sick?"* You need a confident answer. Best answer: *"My husband Prashanth is co-founder & CTO managing tech, ops, and finance — we are converting to Pvt Ltd with a 70:30 split."*

## 3.2 Market Size & Timing (Weight: 15%)

- **TAM > ₹1,000 Cr** for sharks to take you seriously as a venture-scale opportunity.
- **Why now?** What macro tailwind makes this the right moment? (Screen-time crisis, NEP 2020, cultural renaissance.)
- **Bottom-up TAM** beats top-down TAM. (Don't say "Indian kids market is $20B." Say *"50 lakh urban Indian families with kids 1–6, spending ₹2,000/year on books = ₹1,000 Cr SAM."*)

## 3.3 Product & Differentiation / Moat (Weight: 15%)

- **Can a competitor copy this in 6 months?** If yes, you have no moat.
- **Types of moats sharks respect:** brand, IP/patents, distribution lock-in, supply chain, regulatory, network effects, founder/team expertise.
- **Demo > deck.** Sharks LOVE physically holding the product. Bring 4 sets — one for each shark.

**SanskaTots moat narrative:** Brand IP (trademark) + 40-book content library (3 years to copy) + cultural positioning + women-owned MSME advantages.

## 3.4 Traction (Weight: 20%)

The **hardest gate for SanskaTots today.** Traction = revenue, customers, retention, organic growth.

What they want to see:
- **Monthly Recurring Revenue / MRR trend** — is it growing 15%+ MoM?
- **Number of paying customers** (not signups, not visitors).
- **Repeat purchase rate** (>30% = excellent for kids' brands).
- **Organic vs paid mix** — high organic = strong brand, low CAC.
- **Channel diversification** — Amazon-only is a yellow flag. D2C + Amazon + Flipkart is green.

## 3.5 Unit Economics (Weight: 10%)

This is where founders get **destroyed** on the show. Memorize these:

- **CM1 (Contribution Margin 1)** = Revenue – COGS – Direct fulfillment costs
- **CM2** = CM1 – Marketing/CAC
- **CM3** = CM2 – Allocated overhead
- **CAC** = Marketing spend ÷ New paying customers acquired
- **LTV** = (Avg order value × Avg orders per customer per year × Years) × Gross margin %
- **LTV:CAC ratio** — must be >3:1 (sharks want >5:1 for D2C)
- **Payback period** — how many months until CAC is recovered? (Must be <12 months)

**Your SanskaTots numbers from the IM:**
- AOV: ₹449
- COGS + fulfillment: ₹150 (33%)
- Marketing/CAC: ₹67/unit @ 15% ACoS
- CM2: ~₹195–220 (43–49%)
- These are STRONG numbers. Lead with them.

## 3.6 Scalability & Capital Efficiency (Weight: 5%)

- Can ₹1 Cr → ₹50 Cr revenue without ₹50 Cr more capital?
- Asset-light = sharks love it.
- High working capital intensity (inventory, receivables) = yellow flag.

**SanskaTots:** Asset-light (no factory), but inventory-heavy (printed books). Address this proactively.

## 3.7 Defensibility & Exit Path (Weight: 3%)

- Sharks invest to exit in 5–7 years. They want a strategic acquirer or IPO path.
- **Comparable exits in your space:** Skillmatics (raised $20M+, valued ~$200M), Hopscotch (acquired), Firstcry (IPO).
- Mention these as your roadmap.

## 3.8 Valuation Sanity (Weight: 2%)

Even if everything else is perfect, an **insane valuation kills the deal in 2 minutes.** See Section 5.

# 4. THE 7 DEAL-KILLING MISTAKES

Avoid these at all costs. Each one has killed dozens of pitches.

### Mistake #1: Inflated Valuation Without Justification
- Asking ₹2 Cr for 5% (₹40 Cr valuation) when you have ₹0 revenue → instant *"All Sharks Out."*
- **Fix:** Tie valuation to revenue multiple (D2C consumer = 4–8x forward revenue), DCF, or comparable transactions. *Always* have a justification slide ready.

### Mistake #2: Not Knowing Your Numbers
- *"What's your CAC?"* → *"Uh, around... maybe ₹200?"* → DEAD.
- **Fix:** Memorize 15 numbers cold (see Appendix Numbers Card).

### Mistake #3: Lying or Exaggerating
- Sharks have a forensic team. Inflated revenue claims = lawsuit + public humiliation.
- The "Bluepine Foods" and "Sippline" episodes are case studies in what happens when sharks catch you exaggerating.

### Mistake #4: Defensive / Argumentative Posture
- *"Aap samajh nahi rahe ho mera business"* → DEAD.
- **Fix:** Even when a shark is wrong, say *"I see your point — let me clarify with the data."*

### Mistake #5: Founder Disagreement on Stage
- Co-founders contradicting each other in front of cameras = no investment.
- **Fix:** One person speaks per topic. Pre-decide who handles what.

### Mistake #6: Asking Too Little or Too Much
- Asking ₹10L → *"That's not investor money, that's an ICICI loan."*
- Asking ₹5 Cr pre-revenue → *"Are you out of your mind?"*
- **Sweet spot for SanskaTots: ₹50L–₹1 Cr.**

### Mistake #7: No Clear "Why You Need The Money"
- *"For marketing and growth"* = vague = dead.
- **Fix:** Itemized use of funds with milestones. (See Section 9.6.)

# 5. VALUATION — THE MOST CRITICAL NUMBER

## 5.1 The Valuation Formula

```
Valuation = Ask Amount ÷ (Equity Offered ÷ 100)

Example: ₹50 Lakh for 10% → Valuation = ₹50L ÷ 0.10 = ₹5 Crore
```

## 5.2 Valuation Methods Sharks Use

### Method 1: Revenue Multiple (Most Common)
- **D2C consumer brand multiples:** 3–8x **forward (next 12 months) revenue**, 5–15x trailing.
- **Pre-revenue:** Sharks discount HEAVILY — usually 1–3x next year's projected revenue.

**SanskaTots pre-revenue calculation:**
- Year 1 projection: ₹50L–₹1Cr revenue
- Conservative shark valuation: 2x forward = **₹1–2 Cr valuation**
- Aggressive (with great pitch): 4x forward = **₹2–4 Cr valuation**

### Method 2: DCF (Discounted Cash Flow)
- Rarely used on Shark Tank — too complex for 60-min pitch.
- Useful only if you have 3+ years of clean financials.

### Method 3: Comparable Transactions
- *"Skillmatics raised at $200M valuation at ₹X revenue. We are at the same growth rate, so..."*
- Powerful framing **but** Skillmatics had ₹200 Cr+ revenue when valued at $200M. Be careful with comps.

### Method 4: Berkus / Scorecard (Pre-Revenue)
- Ascribes value to: idea (₹0–25L), prototype (₹0–25L), team (₹0–25L), strategic relationships (₹0–25L), product/sales rollout (₹0–25L).
- **SanskaTots Berkus estimate: ₹75L–₹1.25 Cr** valuation pre-revenue.

## 5.3 The "Anchoring" Strategy

**Always anchor higher than your walk-away.** If you want ₹50L for 10% (₹5Cr val), open with:
- **Ask: ₹75L for 10% (₹7.5 Cr valuation)**
- Walk-away: ₹50L for 15% (₹3.3 Cr valuation)
- Sharks will counter — leave room.

## 5.4 The Equity Range — What's Reasonable

| Stage | Reasonable Equity Per Round |
|---|---|
| Pre-revenue / Idea | 8–15% (sharks will push for 20–30%) |
| Early revenue (<₹1Cr ARR) | 5–12% |
| Scaling (₹1–10Cr ARR) | 3–8% |
| Growth (₹10Cr+ ARR) | 1–5% |

**Cardinal rule:** Never give up >25% equity in any single round, or you'll have nothing left after Series A/B dilution.

## 5.5 The 4 Scenarios For Your Pitch

| Scenario | Ask | Equity | Valuation | Likelihood |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Dream Deal** | ₹75L | 10% | ₹7.5 Cr | 5% |
| **Realistic Win** | ₹50L | 12–15% | ₹3.3–4.2 Cr | 25% |
| **Negotiated Down** | ₹50L | 20% + ₹25L debt | ~₹2.5 Cr equity val | 30% |
| **All Sharks Out** | — | — | — | 40% |

> **Mentally pre-commit to your walk-away point BEFORE entering the tank.** Founders who haven't done this lose their shirts in negotiation.

# 6. DEAL STRUCTURES YOU MUST UNDERSTAND

Sharks rarely give you "₹X for Y%" cleanly anymore. Expect creative structures:

## 6.1 Pure Equity
- *"₹50L for 15%."* Simple, founder-friendly if equity ask is low.

## 6.2 Equity + Debt
- *"₹30L equity for 10%, ₹20L as 12% debt repayable in 3 years."*
- **Why sharks like it:** Limits their downside.
- **Founder watch-out:** Debt creates monthly EMIs that strain cashflow.

## 6.3 Royalty Deals
- *"₹50L for 5% royalty on revenue until ₹1.5 Cr returned, then 3% equity."*
- **Aman Gupta's favorite structure.**
- **Watch-out:** Royalty cuts your gross margin by 5% — model the impact carefully.

## 6.4 Convertible (CCD / SAFE)
- Money now, equity decided at next priced round.
- **Founder-friendly** — you don't fix valuation today.

## 6.5 Tranched Investment (Milestone-Based)
- *"₹25L now, ₹25L when you hit ₹50L revenue."*
- Increasingly common. Forces accountability but ties your hands.

## 6.6 Conditional Deals ("Subject to DD")
- Almost ALL Shark Tank deals are conditional. The on-air handshake is non-binding.

## 6.7 The "Strategic Operator" Deal
- Shark wants seat on board, brings supply chain / distribution / hiring help.
- **Highest value-add.** Worth giving extra 2–3% equity for the right strategic shark.

> **For SanskaTots, the IDEAL structure is:** ₹40L equity at ₹4 Cr val (10%) + ₹20L at 10% debt over 24 months + advisory equity 1% vested over 2 years. Total package: ₹60L for 11% effective ownership.

# 7. THE PITCH

## 7.1 The Anatomy of a Winning 90-Second Opener

```
[0–10s]  HOOK — A question, statistic, or contrarian claim that stops sharks from looking at their phones.
[10–30s] PROBLEM — One sentence about the pain. One sentence about who feels it. One sentence about market size.
[30–60s] SOLUTION + DEMO — Show the product. Make a shark touch it.
[60–80s] TRACTION/VALIDATION — Numbers, customers, pre-orders, awards, IP.
[80–90s] THE ASK — "I am here seeking ₹X for Y% equity in [Company]."
```

## 7.2 SanskaTots Opening Script (Memorize Word-for-Word)

> *"Sharks, India's parents are spending ₹85 crore every year on activity books for their kids on Amazon India alone. But not a single bestseller — not Clapjoy, not TodFod, not Little Minds at Work — has any Indian content. No mythology. No Sanskrit shlokas. No Kannada. No Telugu. Nothing Indian.*
>
> *Meanwhile, every Indian mythology book — Wonder House, Amar Chitra Katha — is a flat, boring storybook. No interactivity. No velcro. Nothing for a 2-year-old.*
>
> *I'm Nagarathna, and 18 months ago, I sat with my own kids looking for an activity book that taught them shapes AND Lord Krishna. It didn't exist. So I built it.*
>
> *(Hand each shark a book.)*
>
> *This is SanskaTots. India's first interactive velcro busy book brand with mythology, regional languages, and Indian values. We have 7 products print-ready, 40 more in pipeline, trademark filed, ISBN registered — and we launch on Amazon India in 32 days.*
>
> *I'm here today seeking ₹75 lakhs for 10% equity in Deethya Enterprises — the parent company of SanskaTots."*

## 7.3 Body Language & Voice — The Non-Verbal 70%

- **Walk in confident, not nervous.** Practice the 8-step walk to the carpet 50+ times.
- **Make eye contact with EACH shark for 3–5 seconds during the opener.**
- **Voice modulation** — never monotone. Pause for impact after the ask.
- **Hands open, not crossed.** Crossed arms = defensive = no deal.
- **Smile when answering tough questions.** Sharks test your composure deliberately.
- **Power stance for the ask** — feet shoulder-width, shoulders back, ask delivered slowly.

## 7.4 Wardrobe (Yes, This Matters On TV)

- **Solid colors** that pop on camera (royal blue, deep maroon, mustard, emerald). No checks/stripes (moiré effect on cameras).
- **Brand-aligned**: Wear something that subtly references the brand (saree with Indian motifs reinforces SanskaTots' identity).
- **No logos of other brands.** Sony will blur them.
- **Comfortable footwear** — you may stand for 60+ minutes.

## 7.5 The Demo Kit — Bring This To The Set

1. **4 complete book sets** (one per shark, plus one for table display).
2. **Velcro demo board** — show the interactivity live, let a shark try it.
3. **Side-by-side comparison** — your book vs Clapjoy/LMAW physical samples (300 GSM vs theirs, A4 vs A5).
4. **Customer testimonial cards** (5–10 printed reviews from beta testers — get these by May 25!).
5. **Founder kit board** — large board with: TM certificate, ISBN list, GST cert, MSME cert, sample Amazon listing mockup.
6. **One-page leave-behind** — financials + ask, printed on premium card stock.

# 10. NEGOTIATION TACTICS

## 10.1 The Counter-Offer Playbook

| *"Your numbers are inflated."* | *"Let me walk you through the model."* (Never apologize, justify) |

## 10.2 Bidding War Strategy

If 2+ sharks express interest:
1. **Acknowledge both** — *"Thank you both, this is exciting."*
2. **Ask for the joint deal** — *"Would you two consider partnering on this?"* (Increases your odds of closing post-DD)
3. **Compare value-adds, not just numbers** — *"Aman, what specifically do you bring on Amazon scaling? Namita, what's your view on US market entry via Emcure network?"*
4. **Don't decide on stage if it's close** — *"Can I have 60 seconds to think?"* is allowed and used by smart founders.

## 10.3 The "Walk Away" Power Move

If all offers are bad:
> *"I've put 18 months and ₹10 lakh into this. I came here for ₹75L at ₹7.5 Cr because I believe in the trajectory. The offers on the table dilute me below my comfort. I'd rather walk out with no deal and come back next season at 3x revenue and 2x valuation. Thank you, Sharks."*

This is **respected**, not punished. Multiple founders who walked have come back stronger (Wakefit, BluSmart references).

## 10.4 What NEVER To Negotiate Away

- **Founder vesting >4 years** (you'll get trapped if business pivots)
- **Board seat for shark with veto** (kills agility)
- **Anti-dilution rights ratcheting** (kills future fundraising)
- **ROFR + ROFO together** (you can't sell without their consent)
- **Personal guarantee on shark's investment** (illegal in some structures, never agree)

**Always have a startup lawyer review the SHA before signing post-show.**

## 11.1 Why ~50% of Shark Tank India Deals Fall Through

- **Inflated numbers caught in DD** (founders said ₹50L revenue, actually ₹25L)
- **Trademark / IP disputes** (someone else owns the mark in another class)
- **Founder cap-table issues** (silent partner, undisclosed loan, ESOP overhang)
- **Tax non-compliance** (GST mismatches, TDS dues, ROC filings overdue)
- **Inventory misrepresentation** (claimed 10,000 units in stock, only 3,000)

- **Founder conflict** (co-founders disagree on terms after pitch)
