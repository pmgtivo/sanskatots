# 90-Day Instagram Growth Strategy — SanskaTots

**Created:** June 30, 2026
**Audience:** Indian mothers (toddler + pre-preschool age, tier-1 to tier-3 + NRI)
**North-Star Metric:** Saves + Shares per 1,000 reached
**Strategist Lens:** Every post must earn a **Save** (mom wants to use this later) or a **Share** (mom wants another mom to see this). Likes are vanity; saves and shares are what Instagram's ranking rewards in 2026, and they're the only metrics that predict purchase intent for a considered buy like a ₹400–₹1,200 kids' book.

---

## 1. Content Mix & Reasoning

| Format | Weekly Cadence | % of Output | Job It Does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reels** | 5 / week | ~55% | Reach cold moms outside your follower base. The only format the algorithm pushes to non-followers at scale in 2026. |
| **Carousels** | 3 / week | ~30% | Earn Saves. Carousels are the single highest Save-rate format on IG because moms screenshot/bookmark "how-to" and "checklist" formats. |
| **Stories** | 2–4 / day | ~15% | Warm existing followers, run polls/quizzes, push DMs (which IG now weights heavily as a "deep engagement" signal). Stories don't drive reach — they drive trust and conversion. |

**Why this split:** Reels are top-of-funnel. Carousels are the "save-it-for-bedtime" workhorse — a mom scrolling at 11pm saves a carousel, opens it the next morning, and that's when she clicks the link. Stories are your closer.

**The 60/30/10 content rule inside the mix:**
- 60% **Useful** — solves a parenting micro-problem (e.g., "what to do when your 3yo refuses to sit for 5 minutes")
- 30% **Cultural / Emotional** — pride, nostalgia, identity
- 10% **Product / Buy-now** — direct ask, only after the first two have earned the right

---

## 2. Three 30-Day Phases

### Days 1–30 — "Become the Mom Who Gets It"
Goal: Establish that SanskaTots understands the Indian toddler mom better than any brand she follows.
- Post 80% Useful + Cultural, 20% product
- Pin 3 anchor carousels to the profile (one per Phase 1 book category: Busy, Mythology, Kannada)
- Run one weekly recurring series (see Concept #1) so moms know what to expect on, say, every Tuesday

### Days 31–60 — "Build the Save Habit"
Goal: Train the audience that SanskaTots = the brand they save things from.
- Every carousel ends with a slide saying "Save this for [specific future moment]" — not "save this post"
- Introduce UGC: repost moms using your Velcro books; ask permission, tag, screenshot the DM reaction
- Begin collab Reels with 3–5 micro-mom-creators (5K–30K followers, regional + tier-2 cities — not Mumbai/Delhi lifestyle moms)

### Days 61–90 — "Convert the Quiet Followers"
Goal: Turn saves into purchases.
- Story-led launches: 10-frame story sequences showing one book solving one problem in real time
- Add "comment a word, get the link in DM" Reels — this triggers IG's DM-engagement boost AND captures intent
- Run a "Mom's Verdict" series: hand the book to a real mom on camera, no script, capture the first 8 seconds of her reaction

## 3. Five Proven Content Concepts for the Niche

Repeatable formats, not themes. Run each weekly.

### Concept 1 — "The 7-Minute Fix" (Reel, weekly series)
A 15–25 second Reel showing exactly how a SanskaTots Velcro page buys a mom 7 quiet minutes. Format: chaotic toddler → page placed → toddler engaged → mom sips chai. No voiceover, just text overlay and natural sound.
**Why it works:** sells the outcome (quiet time), not the product. Shares to "the mom group" because every mom wants this.

### Concept 2 — "What I Wish Someone Told Me at Age 2" (Carousel, 8–10 slides)
Written from the mom's POV, not the brand's. Slide 1: a confession ("I thought screen time was harming my 2yo. Turns out the real problem was…"). Slides 2–8: the actual insight (e.g., toddlers need closed-ended activities, not open-ended toys, between 2–3.5 yrs). Slide 9: how a Busy Book applies this. Slide 10: "Save this — you'll need it next week."
**Save rate target:** 8–12%.

### Concept 3 — "Dadi vs. Google" (Reel, 30–45 sec)
Split-screen / two-character format. Google says "introduce phonics at 3." Dadi says "first teach him to fold his hands and say the shloka." Punchline: both are right, and here's the book that does both.
**Why it works:** weaponizes the cultural tension every modern Indian mom lives with — Montessori method vs. sanskaar — and resolves it. Massive share trigger among NRI moms and tier-1 working moms.

### Concept 4 — "The Kannada Test" (Reel, 20 sec) — regional anchor
A toddler is asked to name 5 animals in Kannada (or Hindi/Tamil — rotate). Cut to mom's face when child gets them right. End card: "She learned this from page 11 of [book]."
**Why it works:** regional pride + visible proof + screenshot-worthy moment. Tier-2/tier-3 city share engine.

### Concept 5 — "Bookshelf Audit" (Carousel, 6 slides)
Slide 1: "Open your toddler's bookshelf. Count how many of these 6 things you have." Slides 2–6: each one a category (Velcro activity book, values/mythology book, tracing book, regional language book, board book, story book). Last slide: "Have all 6? You're set. Missing 2+? Here's where to start."
**Why it works:** turns shopping into a checklist, which moms screenshot and forward to their husbands as a justification list.

## 4. Three Core Emotions She Must Feel Before She Buys

Order matters. Every post should be auditable against which emotion it's triggering.

### Emotion 1 — Seen
She has to feel "this brand knows my exact life — the 4pm meltdown, the screen-time guilt, the in-law judgment, the fact that I'm trying to raise a child who's both globally smart and rooted in our culture." Without this, every product post sounds like an ad.
**Content lever:** Concepts 2 and 3.

### Emotion 2 — Relief
She has to feel "finally, someone is making this simple." Indian moms in 2026 are drowning in conflicting advice (Montessori reels, Instagram pediatricians, mom-influencers selling courses). SanskaTots wins when it positions itself as the one shelf that covers everything she's anxious about — early learning, sanskaar, regional identity, screen-free time.
**Content lever:** Concept 5 and pinned anchor carousels.

### Emotion 3 — Pride (not aspiration)
This is the buy trigger. She doesn't want to aspire to be a good mom — she wants to feel she already is one, and the product is the proof she'll show her sister, her mom, and her WhatsApp group. The moment she imagines posting a story of her toddler doing a Velcro page or reciting a shloka, the sale is made.
**Content lever:** Concepts 1 and 4 — both designed to be re-created by the buyer.

> Pride is **not** aspiration. Aspiration sells courses to anxious moms; pride sells products to confident ones. SanskaTots' price point and category demand the latter.

## 5. What to Measure (and Ignore)

Track weekly, in this order:

| Priority | Metric | Day 60 Target |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Saves per 1,000 reached | 15+ |
| 2 | Sends per 1,000 reached | 8+ |
| 3 | Profile visits → follows ratio | 8%+ |
| 4 | DMs initiated from a post or story | True intent signal — track absolute count |
| 5 | Reach + follower count | Outputs, not inputs. Do not optimize directly. |

**Ignore:** likes, raw comment count (unless comments are tagged friends), view count on Reels under 10 seconds.

## 7. Guardrails

- **No generic mom-tropes.** Skip "mom life is hard" / "you got this mama" copy. It's invisible.
- **No stock children.** Every face on the feed should look like the buyer's child.
- **No discount-led posts in the first 60 days.** Discount destroys the pride frame.
- **Regional language captions in alt-text or pinned comment** for every Concept 4 post — this is your tier-2/tier-3 reach unlock.
- **One product per post.** Bundling on the feed kills save rate. Bundle only in stories and DMs.
