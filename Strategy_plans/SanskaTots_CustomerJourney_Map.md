# SanskaTots — Complete Customer Journey Map
**First Touch → Repeat Purchase → Brand Advocate**
*Created: April 2026 | Covers: Urban Indian Parents + NRI Diaspora*

---

## HOW TO READ THIS MAP

Each stage has:
- **Touchpoints** — where the customer interacts with SanskaTots
- **Customer mindset** — what they are thinking/feeling
- **Drop-off risk** — why they might exit the funnel here
- **Content/Action needed** — what we must do to keep them moving forward

---

## STAGE 0 — PRE-AWARENESS (Customer doesn't know us yet)

**Customer state:** Living with the problem but not actively seeking a solution.
> "My 4-year-old is glued to YouTube. I want something hands-on but everything Indian is either boring or cheap-looking."

### Touchpoints
| # | Touchpoint | Channel | Who triggers it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | Scrolling Instagram Reels | Instagram organic | SanskaTots reel auto-plays |
| 0.2 | WhatsApp forward from friend | WhatsApp | Existing customer/follower |
| 0.3 | Sees friend's child using the book | Offline / real world | Word of mouth |
| 0.4 | School teacher mentions it | Offline | B2B school partnership |

### Drop-off risks
- Reel stops within 2 seconds — hook too weak
- Post doesn't reach target audience — wrong hashtags or posting time
- The forward doesn't feel credible — looks like an ad, not a recommendation

### Content/Action needed
- **Hook rule:** First frame must create pattern interrupt. "You've never seen a Kannada book like this" beats "Check out our new book."
- **Reels targeting:** Post 8–10 AM or 6–8 PM IST. Use geo-tagged content (Bengaluru, Mumbai, Hyderabad parents). Mix trending audio with culturally familiar visuals.
- **Reel endings must be shareable:** Every video should end with something a mom wants to send to another mom. "Tag the mom who needs to see this."
- **WhatsApp seeding:** Personally message 100 contacts. Don't broadcast — individual messages only. Ask them to forward to their own parent WhatsApp groups.

## STAGE 1 — AWARENESS (Customer discovers SanskaTots)

**Customer state:** Stopped scrolling. Curious but not convinced.
> "Oh, this looks different. Indian mythology books AND velcro activity pages? I didn't know this existed."

### Touchpoints
| # | Touchpoint | Channel | Trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Watches a Reel to the end | Instagram | Organic or shared |
| 1.2 | Clicks profile to see more content | Instagram | Reel → Profile |
| 1.3 | Sees Amazon PPC ad while searching | Amazon | PPC exact-match |
| 1.4 | Friend mentions it in parent WhatsApp group | WhatsApp | WOM trigger |
| 1.5 | Comes across Amazon listing while browsing | Amazon | Organic search ranking |
| 1.6 | Influencer/nano-creator review post | Instagram/YouTube | Seeded micro-influencer |

### Customer questions at this stage
- "Is this a real brand or some random reseller?"
- "Who made this — are they credible?"
- "Is this good quality or will it fall apart in a week?"

### Drop-off risks
- **Profile visit → no follow:** Grid looks inconsistent; bio is unclear; no content variety
- **Amazon listing click → bounce:** Poor thumbnail, no reviews, cluttered title
- **Influencer post → no action:** Post feels paid/forced; no authentic child reaction shown

### Content/Action needed
- **Instagram bio must answer:** Who made this + for whom + why it's different. Example: *"🇮🇳 Indian activity books for curious kids | Mythology. Languages. Velcro play. | Ship to India & USA | Shop ↓"*
- **Profile grid strategy:** Alternate between: founder story → product demo → relatable mom humor → cultural content → social proof. Never 3 product posts in a row.
- **Amazon listing headline formula:** `[Benefit] | [Key Feature] | [Age Range]` — e.g., *"Kannada 5-in-1 Tracing Book for Kids | Learn Letters with Stories | Age 3–7"*
- **First 3 product images must show:** (a) Book open showing full activity, (b) real child's hands using it, (c) comparison chart showing value vs generic books
- **Launch week:** 15–20 verified reviews minimum before any paid push. No reviews = no conversions.
- **Influencer brief:** Require unscripted child reaction footage. No scripted lines. Authenticity is the only currency.

## STAGE 2 — CONSIDERATION (Customer actively evaluates)

**Customer state:** Has seen SanskaTots at least once. Now actively comparing and researching.
> "Let me check Amazon reviews. How does this compare to what I already buy? Is ₹499 worth it?"

### Touchpoints
| # | Touchpoint | Channel | What they're doing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Reads Amazon reviews in detail | Amazon | Evaluating quality & trust |
| 2.2 | Compares with Clapjoy / TodFod / InIkao | Amazon | Side-by-side comparison |
| 2.3 | Checks Flipkart price | Flipkart | Price validation |
| 2.4 | DMs Instagram asking questions | Instagram DMs | Personal validation |
| 2.5 | Searches YouTube for an unboxing | YouTube | Tactile/quality research |
| 2.6 | Asks in a mom Facebook/WhatsApp group | Social groups | Peer recommendation |
| 2.7 | Checks for seller credibility on Amazon | Amazon storefront | Trust check |
| 2.8 | Looks at shipping time & return policy | Amazon / Website | Risk management |

### Customer questions at this stage
- "What do other parents say about the quality?"
- "Is the Kannada actually correct / well-designed?"
- "Will my 3-year-old actually use this or leave it in a corner?"
- "What happens if it's damaged — can I return it?"
- "Is this the same price everywhere?"

### Drop-off risks
- **Review volume too low:** Fewer than 20 reviews = buyer hesitation is extremely high
- **Competitor listing is stronger:** Better images, more reviews, lower price, Prime badge
- **DM goes unanswered or answered late:** Kills the moment of purchase intent
- **No YouTube/unboxing content:** Research-heavy buyers need video proof
- **Price mismatch across channels:** Confuses and creates distrust
- **No answers to age-specific questions:** Parent of a 2-year-old needs confirmation the book works for their child

### Content/Action needed
- **Review seeding (Month 1):** Friends & family blitz = 50–75 purchases + explicit request for detailed review mentioning (a) quality of velcro, (b) Indian/cultural content, (c) child's reaction. Never ask for "5 stars" — ask for "honest experience."
- **Amazon A+ Content:** Build comparison module — SanskaTots vs generic activity books. Show: materials, page count, cultural depth, reusability. Never name competitors; let the comparison speak.
- **Q&A section (Amazon):** Pre-seed 10–15 answered questions covering: age suitability, velcro durability, return policy, language correctness, print quality.
- **Instagram DMs:** Set up Quick Replies for top 5 questions. Response time SLA = under 2 hours during 9 AM–9 PM. Miss the window, miss the sale.
- **Pricing parity:** Same price on Amazon India and Flipkart. WhatsApp D2C can be same or offer a bundle deal — never cheaper on one public channel.
- **YouTube unboxing:** Send free copies to 3–5 parenting YouTubers (even with 1K–5K subscribers). 20-minute unboxing = the most powerful trust-builder that exists.
- **Amazon storefront:** Build a clean brand storefront showing product categories, origin story, and "Why SanskaTots" banner. This is the landing page for anyone clicking the brand name.

## STAGE 3 — PURCHASE DECISION (Moment of truth)

**Customer state:** Convinced in principle. Now looking for a reason to click "Add to Cart" right now.
> "Okay, I want this. But should I wait for a deal? Should I get the bundle or just one book first?"

### Touchpoints
| # | Touchpoint | Channel | Friction to solve |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Amazon listing — Add to Cart | Amazon | Price/value hesitation |
| 3.2 | Applies Amazon coupon | Amazon | Discount motivation |
| 3.3 | WhatsApp D2C order via chat | WhatsApp | Ease of ordering |
| 3.4 | Sees Lightning Deal / Limited time offer | Amazon | Urgency trigger |
| 3.5 | Bundle recommendation at checkout | Amazon | AOV increase |
| 3.6 | Influencer discount code used | Instagram → Amazon | Trust + incentive combined |
| 3.7 | Friend's recommendation with buy link | WhatsApp | Referral trust |
| 3.8 | School places bulk order | Direct / Email | B2B pathway |

### Customer questions at this stage
- "Is there a coupon or better deal somewhere?"
- "Should I get just one book or a bundle — is the bundle actually cheaper?"
- "How long will delivery take? I need it before [event/birthday]."
- "What if my child doesn't like it?"

### Drop-off risks
- **No urgency:** Without a reason to buy today, decision gets deferred and forgotten
- **Cart abandoned due to shipping time:** 7+ day delivery for a gifting occasion = lost sale
- **Bundle value not clear:** If savings aren't shown explicitly, customer defaults to single book
- **Payment friction:** COD unavailable, UPI glitch, or card decline with no follow-up
- **Return policy anxiety:** "What if the velcro breaks?" — unaddressed = lost purchase

### Content/Action needed
- **Always have an active Amazon coupon:** 10–15% clip coupon on every listing, always on. This shows up in search results and increases CTR by 20–30%. Never let it lapse.
- **Bundle framing in listing:** Show strikethrough price: ~~₹747~~ → ₹499 Mythology Bundle. Savings must be in ₹ and %, not vague "great value."
- **Lightning Deals rhythm:** Thursday and Sunday evenings, 7–9 PM IST. Apply 10 days in advance. Run 2–3 per week during ramp-up, 4–5 per week in October–November.
- **Shipping message on listing:** "Order before 2 PM → Ships same day" (during Easy Ship) / "Prime — Delivered in 1–2 days" (after FBA switch). Delivery speed anxiety is real.
- **Return policy:** Explicitly state "Easy 7-day return" in bullet points of listing. Not just in T&C — in the product bullets where eyes go.
- **WhatsApp D2C flow:** Message template: "Hi! Here's your direct order link for [Book Name] + bundle: [link]. I'll personally confirm your order. We ship within 24 hours." Make it feel personal, not automated.
- **Influencer codes:** Each micro-influencer gets a unique 10% code (e.g., PRIYA10). Trackable, creates urgency, gives buyer a "win."
- **Post-cart-abandon (WhatsApp):** If customer DM'd asking about a product and didn't order — follow up once after 48 hours: "Hey! Did you get a chance to check out the Mythology Bundle? A few parents in Bengaluru ordered last week — happy to answer any questions."

## STAGE 4 — FIRST PURCHASE (Transaction complete)

**Customer state:** Ordered. Excited but also anxious about what they'll receive.
> "Hope it's as good as it looks in the photos. My daughter's going to love the Ramayana one."

### Touchpoints
| # | Touchpoint | Channel | Experience quality |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | Order confirmation | Amazon / WhatsApp | Automated but must feel good |
| 4.2 | Shipping notification | Amazon / courier | Expectation management |
| 4.3 | Unboxing the physical product | Physical | THE moment of truth |
| 4.4 | First use by the child | Physical | Product delivers on promise |
| 4.5 | Amazon review request email | Amazon / follow-up | Feedback collection |

### Customer questions at this stage
- "When will this arrive?"
- "Is the packaging nice — it's a gift for my niece."
- "Is the quality what I saw in the Instagram video?"

### Drop-off risks
- **Delivery delay:** Longer than stated = negative first impression before product is even opened
- **Poor unboxing experience:** Damaged book, loose velcro, missing pieces, cheap-feeling packaging = instant regret
- **Mismatch between reel content and actual product:** Overpromised visuals vs real pages = returns + 1-star reviews
- **No follow-through communication:** Customer never hears from you again → no review, no repeat

### Content/Action needed
- **Packaging as a touchpoint:** Wrap books in tissue paper inside a SanskaTots-branded mailer bag. Include a handwritten (or printed as handwritten) thank-you card: *"Thank you for choosing Indian. Your child is lucky to have a parent like you. — Prashanth & Team SanskaTots"*
- **Insert card in every package:**
  - How to use the velcro book (quick tips)
  - QR code linking to an Instagram Reel showing activity ideas
  - Request for Amazon review: *"If your child loves it, a 2-minute review helps other Indian parents find us."*
- **For WhatsApp D2C orders:** Send a personal message when item ships: "Your [Book Name] is on its way! Tracking: [link]. Can't wait to hear what your little one thinks 😊" — then follow up 5–7 days after expected delivery.
- **Quality control non-negotiables:** Every velcro book checked before packing. Spine must be reinforced. Pages must lay flat. Printing must have no colour registration errors. One bad product review shared on Instagram = 10 lost customers.
- **Amazon review automation:** Use Amazon's "Request a Review" button 7 days post-delivery. Do this manually for first 3 months; automate once on FBA.

## STAGE 5 — POST-PURCHASE EXPERIENCE (Day 1–30 after delivery)

**Customer state:** Product is in home. Either delighted, satisfied, or disappointed.
> "She's been playing with the velcro animals for 20 minutes. Haven't seen that from a book before."

### Touchpoints
| # | Touchpoint | Channel | Opportunity |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | Child using the book (repeated) | Physical | Core value delivery |
| 5.2 | Parent shares photo/video of child | WhatsApp / Instagram | UGC creation moment |
| 5.3 | Parent mentions it in a parent group | WhatsApp group | WOM seed |
| 5.4 | Visits Instagram for more content | Instagram | Retention hook |
| 5.5 | Receives WhatsApp nurture message | WhatsApp | Relationship building |
| 5.6 | Sees new SanskaTots reel on Instagram | Instagram | Re-engagement |

### Drop-off risks
- **Child plays once, then ignores it:** Poor activity design or wrong age-level = no repeat purchase
- **Parent doesn't share/review:** No prompt, no action — missed UGC and review opportunity
- **No further brand contact:** Customer liked it but forgets about us — no follow-up pull
- **Negative experience not caught early:** 1-star review posted without brand getting chance to resolve

### Content/Action needed
- **WhatsApp follow-up sequence (D2C customers):**
  - Day 7: "Hi! Has [child's name] started using the book? We'd love to see a photo if you're comfortable sharing!"
  - Day 14: "Quick tip: for the velcro animals, try asking your child to sort them by habitat — it turns into a 30-minute activity! 🐘🦁"
  - Day 21: "We just launched the Telugu Tracing Book — thought of you since you loved the Kannada one. Want to see it?"
- **UGC capture strategy:** In insert card: *"Share your child's reaction and tag @SanskaTots — we'll reshare on our page and send you a surprise discount for your next order."*
- **Instagram content to retain:** Post "how to use" content, "activity extension ideas," and "what your child is learning" educational posts. Parent watches and re-engages with brand.
- **Negative feedback interception:** Monitor Amazon reviews daily. If 1-star or 2-star posted, respond publicly within 24 hours: acknowledge, offer resolution. DM them privately. Convert upset customers — they become the most vocal advocates once resolved.

## STAGE 6 — REPEAT PURCHASE (The real business begins here)

**Customer state:** Has had a good experience. Now open to buying again — needs a trigger.
> "She's finished all the activities in the Kannada book. I wonder what else SanskaTots has."

### Touchpoints
| # | Touchpoint | Channel | Trigger type |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6.1 | Sees new product launch reel | Instagram | New SKU awareness |
| 6.2 | Receives WhatsApp message about new book | WhatsApp | Direct outreach |
| 6.3 | Gets seasonal offer (Diwali, summer) | WhatsApp / Amazon | Seasonal urgency |
| 6.4 | Amazon "frequently bought together" | Amazon | Platform recommendation |
| 6.5 | Friend asks "which SanskaTots book should I get?" | WOM | Social proof trigger |
| 6.6 | School starts (Jan/June) back-to-school pull | Seasonal intent | Timing alignment |
| 6.7 | Child's birthday approaching | Offline context | Gift-buying occasion |

### Customer questions at this stage
- "What else do they have that my child would like at this age?"
- "Is there a bundle deal for buying 3–4 books?"
- "I loved the Kannada one — do they have Telugu for my cousin's child?"

### Drop-off risks
- **No new products to buy:** Catalogue too small in early months
- **No reminder mechanism:** Customer willing to buy but no trigger at the right moment
- **Price creep without perceived value increase:** "It's ₹50 more than last time, why?"
- **Child has outgrown the product range:** No product for the next age group yet

### Content/Action needed
- **WhatsApp community (the most powerful retention tool):** By Month 3, build a community of 200–500 buyers. Weekly value drop: a parenting tip, an activity idea, a cultural story. Monthly: exclusive early access to new products. Quarterly: community-only flash sale.
- **"Complete the collection" framing:** After first purchase, Amazon listing cross-sells: *"Customers who bought the Kannada book also got the Ramayana Stories — complete the learning journey."*
- **Seasonal calendar (plan 60 days ahead):**
  - Jan–Feb: School admission season → "Start the year with Indian roots"
  - March–April: Summer holiday → "Screen-free summer activity pack"
  - Oct–Nov: Diwali → "The gift every Indian child deserves"
  - Dec: Christmas/Year-end → "Gift an Indian experience"
- **Loyalty mechanism:** Simple punch card concept via WhatsApp: "Buy 5 books, get 1 free." Track manually via WhatsApp chat history. No app needed at this stage.
- **New SKU launch to existing base first:** Every new book goes to WhatsApp community 48 hours before Amazon listing goes live. This creates a "VIP early access" feeling at zero extra cost.

## STAGE 7 — ADVOCACY (Customer becomes unpaid sales force)

**Customer state:** Loves the brand. Proud to recommend it. Connects with the cultural mission.
> "I told three moms about SanskaTots. One already ordered. This is exactly what Indian kids need."

### Touchpoints
| # | Touchpoint | Channel | What's happening |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7.1 | Posts child using book on Instagram | Instagram | Organic UGC |
| 7.2 | Shares reel to parent WhatsApp group | WhatsApp | Network amplification |
| 7.3 | Tags SanskaTots in post | Instagram | Brand mention |
| 7.4 | Writes detailed Amazon review with story | Amazon | Social proof creation |
| 7.5 | Recommends in mom Facebook groups | Facebook groups | Community influence |
| 7.6 | Refers using affiliate/discount code | WhatsApp / Instagram | Trackable referral |
| 7.7 | Schools recommend to parent community | Offline / WhatsApp | B2B → B2C bridge |

### Drop-off risks
- **Great experience but no channel to share it:** No easy way to tag, share, or refer
- **Referral feels awkward without incentive:** "I don't want to seem like I'm earning money by recommending this"
- **Brand stops posting / goes quiet:** Advocate disengages when brand feels inactive
- **Advocate not publicly acknowledged:** Their post shared without a thank-you = feels transactional

### Content/Action needed
- **Make advocacy easy and natural:** Caption template on every post: *"Know a mom who would love this? Tag her below — she deserves to see this."*
- **UGC resharing ritual:** Every customer photo/video reshared on Stories within 24 hours of it being posted. DM the customer: *"We just reshared your post — your little one is our brand ambassador now 💛"*
- **Referral program (simple, no app needed):**
  - Customer gets a WhatsApp message: *"You've ordered X times — thank you! Here's your personal code: PRIYA15. When a friend uses it, they get 15% off, and we'll send you ₹100 off your next order."*
  - Track via unique codes, honour manually. Keep it human.
- **Community influencer identification:** Spot advocates who forward your content regularly. Invite them into a "SanskaTots Parent Circle" — first to see new products, early access pricing, get 5 free copies to give away in their networks.
- **Spotlight advocates on your feed:** Monthly "Parent of the Month" post featuring a real family story. This turns one advocate into a billboard that reaches all their followers.

## TOP 5 HIGHEST-IMPACT INTERVENTIONS (Across entire journey)

1. **Review seeding in Week 1 (Stage 2 → 3):** Without 20+ reviews at launch, every other investment is wasted. Friends & family blitz is not optional — it is the prerequisite for everything else working.

2. **WhatsApp community by Month 2 (Stage 5 → 6 → 7):** This is the highest-margin, highest-loyalty channel. ₹0 platform fee. Direct relationship. Every repeat buyer should be in this community within 7 days of first purchase.

3. **Packaging insert card (Stage 4 → 5 → 7):** One card inside every package drives reviews, UGC, and repeat purchases. Cost: ₹3–5 per card. Return: measurable review uplift, UGC posts, and referrals.

4. **Lightning deals + coupon rhythm (Stage 3):** Purchase decisions need external urgency. A buyer who is 70% convinced becomes 100% convinced when they see "9 left in stock" or a 7-hour sale countdown.

5. **DM response speed (Stage 2 → 3):** Every unanswered DM is a lost ₹500. Respond within 2 hours during waking hours. Set up Quick Replies on Instagram and WhatsApp Business. A fast, warm personal reply converts at 40–60%.
