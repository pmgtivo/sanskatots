# SanskaTots™ — English Language Learning Ladder, Age 2 → 8
## Stage-by-stage: from listening and pre-writing → letters → phonics → sight words → word families → fluent reading, grammar and writing
**Prepared:** 13 Nov 2026 | **Companion to:** `SanskaTots_EarlyChildhood_Age2to6_NCERT_Pediatric_Psychology_Report_Nov2026.md`

> **Basis & confidence:** This ladder is my synthesis of widely used early-literacy frameworks: the "science of reading" consensus (the Simple View of Reading), Jolly Phonics, UK *Letters and Sounds* (Phases 1–6), Dolch/Fry sight-word lists, Common Core foundational skills (K–2) and the NCF-FS goals CG-9, CG-10, CG-11. I wrote it from established knowledge and did not look these sources up this session. The ordering is well established, but the **age bands are approximate and mastery-based** — a child moves up when skills are secure, not when the birthday arrives. Verify word lists and reading-speed numbers against the primary sources (Part 9) before printing them on a pack.

# PART 1 — THE BIG PICTURE

## 1.1 Reading = two ropes twisted together (Scarborough's Reading Rope / Simple View of Reading)
| Rope | Strands | Taught by |
|---|---|---|
| **Language comprehension** (understanding) | Vocabulary, background knowledge, grammar, story structure, inference | Talking, read-aloud, songs, stories, play |
| **Word recognition** (decoding) | Phonological awareness, letter knowledge, phonics/decoding, sight-word recognition | Explicit, systematic phonics + practice |
| **Result** | **Reading comprehension = Decoding × Language comprehension** | Both must grow; a child can decode but not understand, or understand but not decode |

**Implication:** a good English ladder is **not just phonics worksheets**. Every stage below has a **Listen/Speak** track (language rope), a **Read** track (decoding), and a **Write** track (motor + spelling + composition).

## 1.2 The 3 tracks every stage includes
1. **Listen & Speak** — oral language, vocabulary, rhymes, stories
2. **Read** — phonological awareness → letters → phonics → sight words → fluency → comprehension
3. **Write** — pre-writing → letter formation → words → sentences → paragraphs

## 1.3 The ladder at a glance
| Stage | Age (approx.) | Name | Main milestone |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | 1½–2½ | Listen & Talk | Understands and names ~50–200+ everyday words; loves rhymes/books |
| **1** | 2–3 | Pre-Reading & Pre-Writing | Handles books, scribbles, copies lines, hears rhymes, sees letters in the environment |
| **2** | 3–4 | Letter Awareness | Sound play (rhyme, syllables, first sounds), knows many letters, traces strokes/shapes, writes first name (maybe) |
| **3** | 4–5 | Letter-Sound Mastery | Knows all 26 letter **sounds** and shapes, forms letters, hears first/last sounds, blends 2–3 sounds |
| **4** | 5–6 | First Decoding | Reads **CVC** words and **word families**, first sight words, simple decodable sentences, writes CVC words |
| **5** | 6–7 | Expanding Phonics | Digraphs, blends, long vowels (magic e), vowel teams; sight words Pre-primer → Grade 1; reads short books; writes sentences |
| **6** | 7–8 | Fluent Reader & Writer | Multisyllable words, affixes, grammar basics, fluency, comprehension, paragraph writing, cursive |

> **NCF-FS note:** The framework prefers **oral English (L2) through pre-school**, mother-tongue-first literacy (L1) and **no formal reading/writing drills before Class 1**. For a child whose home language isn't English, treat Stages 0–3 as **oral + play**, begin English print (Stage 4) around age 5½–6+, and allow 6–12 extra months of oral vocabulary before expecting Stage 5–6 comprehension. See Part 7.

## STAGE 0 — Listen & Talk (age ~1½–2½)
**Goal:** build the vocabulary and love of language that all reading depends on. *No letters needed.*

| Track | Concepts / skills |
|---|---|
| **Listen & Speak** | Names common objects, body parts, family, foods, animals, vehicles; understands "in/on/under"; follows 1-step then 2-step instructions; uses 2-word phrases ("more milk"); points to named pictures; asks simple questions; copies animal/vehicle sounds |
| **Concepts of print (early)** | Knows a book has a front and back; turns pages (board books); points at pictures |
| **Read** | Shared picture reading, repetition books; lap reading with talk |
| **Write** | Fist-grasp scribbling; pat, squeeze, poke play |
| **Vocabulary targets** | ~50 words at 24 mo, rapidly growing to 200–300 by ~30–36 mo |

**Activities:** picture-book naming ("Where's the dog?"), action rhymes, finger plays, singing, labelling things in the home, sensory bins with naming, "I spy" with objects, pretend play.
**Key adult moves:** *expand* ("Dog!" → "Yes, a big brown dog!"), ask open questions, wait for answers, narrate routines.
**Pitfalls:** screens for language (WHO/IAP recommend none <2 yr); asking the child to "repeat the alphabet" — premature and not helpful.

## STAGE 1 — Pre-Reading & Pre-Writing (age ~2–3)
**Goal:** books, sounds and hands get ready.

| Track | Concepts / skills |
|---|---|
| **Listen & Speak** | 2–3 word sentences → short sentences; uses "me/mine/my"; names colours, shapes, animals; learns nursery rhymes; says own name; asks "what's that?" |
| **Concepts of print** | Book orientation (right way up), turning pages one at a time, pictures vs words |
| **Phonological awareness** | Listens to rhymes, enjoys rhyme and rhythm, claps along with songs |
| **Letter knowledge** | Exposure only: sees letters in name, signs, packaging; may recognise first letter of own name; sings the Alphabet Song (ok, but not the learning target) |
| **Write (pre-writing)** | Scribble → vertical line (~2 yr) → horizontal line (~2½) → circle (~3). Grasp progresses fisted → digital-pronate |
| **Fine motor** | Stack 4–6 blocks, turn pages, peel stickers, play-dough, large beads, pegs |

**Activities:** read-alouds with repeated lines; *pointing and naming* books (My First Words); Velcro matching (picture-picture, picture-shape); big crayons, finger paint, chalk, sand/salt tray drawing; "what sound does the cow make?"; sensory trays with letters just for exposure.
**Checkpoints (by ~3):** names ≥ 8–10 body parts, ≥ 6 colours or shapes (soon), joins in rhymes, copies vertical/horizontal line, circle scribble.
**Pitfalls:** pencil + worksheets now (hands not ready, motivation drops); rushing "A for Apple" rote chant with no meaning.

## STAGE 2 — Letter Awareness & Sound Play (age ~3–4)
**Goal:** hear the sounds *inside* spoken words (phonological awareness) and start to recognise letters.

### 2.1 Phonological awareness (PA) — ORAL, no letters needed
Teach in this order (easiest → hardest):
1. **Environmental sounds** (listen walks, "what do you hear?")
2. **Rhyme** — recognise ("Do cat and hat rhyme?"), then produce ("What rhymes with sun?")
3. **Syllable clapping** — "ba-na-na" (3 claps)
4. **Alliteration / initial sound** — "Which starts with /s/: sun or moon?"
5. *(Next stage)* blending and segmenting individual phonemes

### 2.2 Letter knowledge
| Concept | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Letter names vs letter sounds** | Teach **both**; research suggests *sounds are the decoding tool*; names help too. Start with the letters in the child's name, then high-value letters (S, A, T, I, P, N, M, B, C, R) |
| **Uppercase first** | Uppercase letters are easier for the hand (straight lines, circles); introduce lowercase after (Stage 3) |
| **Recognition before writing** | Match, find, sort, trace with finger on sand, air-write; **recognise ~10–20 letters** by 4 |
| **Avoid** | A-to-Z in a row; teach by **shape-and-stroke families** and by usefulness (see Part 4) |

### 2.3 Write
- Pre-writing strokes: **cross (+) ~4 yr**, square ~4½; trace lines (vertical, horizontal, slanting, zig-zag, curves, loops, waves); **wavy, scallop, spiral**.
- First letters: line/curve letters (**L, I, T, H, E, F**; then **C, O, U, S** etc.). **Own first name** in uppercase/Title case.
- Grasp: quadrupod/static tripod emerging.

### 2.4 Listen & Speak
- Sentences of 3–5 words → conversations; tells what happened; "who/what/where/why" questions; learns opposites, colours, numbers, categories (fruits, animals); uses "I/you/he/she"; plurals in speech (dogs); simple past in speech ("went").
- Story: sequence 3 pictures; retell favourite story.

### 2.5 Vocabulary themes
Family, body, food, animals, home, transport, clothes, school, weather, feelings, colours, shapes, numbers, opposites (big/small, hot/cold…).

**Checkpoints (by ~4):** recognises several letters (own name's); claps syllables; recognises/produces simple rhymes; draws circle/cross; speaks in sentences; retells a short story with help.
**Activities:** letter hunts, alphabet sensory bins, play-dough letters, tracing letters in sand/rice, magnetic letters, **sound I-Spy** ("I spy something that starts with /m/"), **rhyming-picture matching**, name puzzles, **Velcro letter-picture matching**, stories + puppets.
**Pitfalls:** forcing handwriting rows; teaching whole alphabet in order; "cap and small together" too early; confusing PA with phonics (PA is oral).

## STAGE 3 — Letter-Sound Mastery & Early Writing (age ~4–5)
**Goal:** knows **all 26 letter sounds** (and the shapes), writes letters correctly, **blends sounds** into simple words orally.

### 3.1 Phonics order (use ONE systematic order; do not improvise)
**Jolly Phonics 42 sounds (groups):**
1. **s a t i p n**
2. **c/k e h r m d**
3. **g o u l f b**
4. **ai j oa ie ee or**
5. **z w ng v oo/oo y x**
6. **ch sh th th qu ou oi ue er ar**

**Letters and Sounds Phase 2 (equivalent order):** Set 1 s a t p → Set 2 i n m d → Set 3 g o c k → Set 4 ck e u r → Set 5 h b f ff l ll ss.
**Why this order?** The first sets let kids build many words fast (sat, pin, tap, sip, nap, pat, tip…) and avoid confusable letters appearing together (b/d, m/n, p/q).

### 3.2 Concepts
| Skill | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Letter sound** (not letter *name*) | /s/ not "ess"; pure sounds (avoid adding "uh": /s/, not "suh") |
| **Blending** | s-a-t → "sat" (oral, then with letters) |
| **Segmenting** | "sat" → /s/ /a/ /t/ (finger tapping, Elkonin boxes) |
| **Initial/final sounds** | Which sound is first/last? |
| **Uppercase + lowercase** | Learn lowercase *by sound* (reading); uppercase matched later |
| **Concepts of print** | Left-to-right, top-to-bottom, word spaces, sentence/word/letter, title/author |

### 3.3 Write
- **Letter formation by stroke family** (reduces reversals):
  - *Curly caterpillar:* **c a d g o q** (start with c)
  - *Long ladder:* **l i t j u y**
  - *One-armed robot:* **r n m h b p**
  - *Zig-zag monster:* **z v w x k**
  - Others: s, e, f
- Writes own name; copies words (CVC) from model; **sound-spelling** ("invented spelling" is fine, encourages segmenting).
- Tracing on **rows** (lines: top, middle, baseline — "sky, grass, ground") begins; sizes ~1–1.5 cm.
- **Pencil grasp:** moving to dynamic tripod; **scissors** cutting lines/shapes; clay, tongs.

### 3.4 Listen & Speak
- Retells stories (beginning/middle/end), asks and answers questions, understands "why" and "how", uses **tenses**: past ("walked"), future ("will go"); learns **vocabulary themes** (community helpers, seasons, feelings, transport, food…).
- **Rhyming and word play** continues: rhyme production, odd-one-out, syllables, sound deletion later.

**Checkpoints (by ~5):** knows ≥ 20 letter sounds (all 26 by 5½); forms most letters; hears and says first/last sounds; blends 2–3 sounds orally; writes name; knows ~5–10 simple sight words by sight (I, a, the, to, and, my, is, in, it) in context.

**Activities:** letter-of-the-week with **sound + action** (Jolly Phonics actions), sandpaper letters, sound-picture cards, **magnetic letter building**, "read the room", blending games with cards, **Velcro letter→picture/CVC building**, mini whiteboard/write-wipe tracing, sorting by initial sound.
**Pitfalls:** only singing the alphabet without sounds; **b/d reversals are normal** until ~7 — give a visual anchor (e.g., bed hand-trick) and stroke-family formation; uppercase-only reading; skipping blending.

## STAGE 4 — First Decoding: CVC, Word Families, First Sight Words (age ~5–6)
**Goal:** reads and writes **simple decodable words and sentences**. This is the **core "phonics" stage**.

### 4.1 Phonics ladder (decoding)
| Step | Concept | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 4a | **CVC** short **a** words | cat, mat, bat, sat, man, pan, cap, map, bag, tap |
| 4b | CVC short **i** | pin, sit, pig, big, lip, hit, bin |
| 4c | CVC short **o** | dog, pot, hop, top, log, hot, mop |
| 4d | CVC short **e** | pen, hen, net, bed, red, leg, jet |
| 4e | CVC short **u** | sun, cup, bug, bus, nut, mud, run |
| 4f | **Mixed short vowels** (contrast) | cat/cut, pin/pen, hot/hit |
| 4g | **Word families** (rime patterns) | see Part 3 |
| 4h | **Double final consonants** (FLoSS rule: ff, ll, ss, zz) | bell, hill, doll, cuff, kiss, buzz |
| 4i | **Final "ck"** | back, sock, duck, kick |
| 4j | Extend to **CVCC / CCVC** with common blends | tent, lamp, hand, jump, stop, frog, flag, clap |

### 4.2 Sight words (high-frequency / "tricky" words)
- **What:** words that appear constantly (the, was, said, you) — some are **decodable** (it, in, and), others have **irregular parts** ("heart words": said, was, the).
- **Teach:** map the sounds that *are* regular, then flag the irregular part ("s-ai-d → 'ai' says /e/ here"), rather than only "memorise by shape".
- **List:** **Dolch Pre-primer (40)**, then **Primer (52)**; or **Fry first 100**. (Part 4 lists them.)
- **Goal by end of stage:** **~40–100** automatically, in context of short sentences.

### 4.3 Reading
- **Decodable readers** (books made only of the sounds/words taught so far) — **Level 1 → 3**.
- **Sentence reading:** "The cat sat on a mat." → "Sam has a red cap."
- **Print concepts:** capital letter at the start; full stop at the end; spaces between words.
- **Fluency start:** accurate, then smooth; **re-read** same short text 2–3 times.
- **Comprehension:** answer "who/what/where" about a short sentence/picture; predict; sequence story pictures; retell.

### 4.4 Write
- Writes **CVC words** by sound-spelling; **dictation** of simple words, then **short sentences** (3–5 words).
- **Capital letter + full stop**; spaces; uses the **word wall** for sight words.
- **Handwriting:** correct formation of **all lowercase**; sizes consistent; on **4-line** or **2-line** notebook ruling.
- **Uppercase** for names/sentence start.

### 4.5 Language
Singular/plural (cat→cats), **opposites**, **action words (verbs)**, **naming words (nouns)**, **describing words (adjectives)** — all *orally and with pictures*, not as grammar terminology tests.

**Checkpoints (by ~6):** reads CVC words and 3–6-word decodable sentences; spells simple CVC words; ~40–100 sight words; writes a simple sentence with capital and full stop; retells a short story.
**Activities:** **word-family sliders/wheels**, flip books, Velcro build-a-word (c-a-t), picture-to-word matching, **sentence strips**, Elkonin boxes, reading games (bingo, sort, hunt), whiteboard writing, **write-wipe-repeat** word tracing, short **decodable** mini-books.
**Pitfalls:** guessing from pictures instead of decoding; teaching **word families before CVC blending is solid**; skipping sight-word *meaning*; moving to long vowels too soon.

## STAGE 5 — Expanding Phonics & Early Fluency (age ~6–7)
**Goal:** reads **unfamiliar words** with common spelling patterns and short chapter-style books.

### 5.1 Phonics ladder
| Step | Concept | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 5a | **Consonant digraphs** (two letters, one sound) | **sh** (ship), **ch** (chip), **th** (this/thin), **wh** (when), **ph** (phone), **ng** (ring), **nk** (sink), **ck** |
| 5b | **Initial blends** | **bl, cl, fl, gl, pl, sl** (L-blends); **br, cr, dr, fr, gr, pr, tr** (R-blends); **sc, sk, sm, sn, sp, st, sw** (S-blends); **tw**; **spr, str, scr, spl** (3-letter) |
| 5c | **Final blends** | -nd, -nk, -nt, -mp, -st, -sk, -ft, -lt, -lk, -ld, -lp |
| 5d | **Long vowels with silent e** (a_e, i_e, o_e, u_e, e_e) | cake, bike, home, cube, Pete |
| 5e | **Vowel teams (long a)** | ai (rain), ay (day), a_e |
| 5f | **Vowel teams (long e)** | ee (tree), ea (leaf), e |
| 5g | **Vowel teams (long i)** | igh (night), ie (pie), y (my) |
| 5h | **Vowel teams (long o)** | oa (boat), ow (snow), o_e |
| 5i | **Long u / oo** | ue (blue), ew (new), oo (moon), u_e (cube); short **oo** (book) |
| 5j | **R-controlled vowels** | ar (car), or (fork), er/ir/ur (her/bird/fur), air/ear |
| 5k | **Diphthongs** | ou/ow (cloud/cow), oi/oy (coin/boy), aw/au (saw/haul) |
| 5l | **Soft c / soft g** | city, cent, giant, gem |
| 5m | **Silent letters (intro)** | kn (knee), wr (write), mb (lamb), gn |
| 5n | **-ed / -ing endings** (+ doubling & drop-e rules) | jumped, hopping, baking |
| 5o | **Plurals** -s, -es, -ies | cats, boxes, babies |
| 5p | **Compound words, contractions (intro)** | sunset, can't |

### 5.2 Sight words
- **Dolch Primer → Grade 1** (and/or **Fry 100–200**), plus **tricky words** (the, said, was, they, one, two, were, there, their, come, some, could…).
- **Goal:** automatic recognition of **~150–220** words.

### 5.3 Reading
- **Leveled/decodable books** with **short stories** (50–150 words).
- **Fluency:** read aloud smoothly with expression; **phrasing** (punctuation, question marks, exclamation marks).
- **Comprehension strategies:** **who/what/where/when/why**; sequence; **main idea** (what is it mostly about?); **characters/setting**; **predict**; **cause–effect**; **compare** two stories.
- **Genres:** story, rhyme, simple non-fiction, **fable**, **poem**.

### 5.4 Write
- Writes **sentences** from own ideas: capital letter, spaces, **full stop / question mark / exclamation**.
- **Spelling:** high-frequency words, phonic patterns, **dictation** sentences.
- **Creative writing (guided):** 3–5 sentence stories/diary; **labelling**, **captions**; **picture-prompt** writing.
- **Handwriting:** neat lowercase with ascenders/descenders; correct **spacing**; consistent size; **cursive intro** at the school's approach (often Class 2/3).

### 5.5 Grammar & Language (introduce informally)
- **Nouns** (naming), **verbs** (action), **adjectives** (describing) — sort, circle, use.
- **Articles a/an/the**; **singular/plural**; **pronouns** (he/she/it/they); **simple prepositions** (in, on, under, behind, between); **opposites**; **simple past vs present** in sentences.
- **Punctuation:** capital letters (names, I, start), full stop, question mark, exclamation mark, commas in lists.

**Checkpoints (by ~7):** reads unfamiliar CVC/CCVC/CVCC words and many long-vowel words; reads a short story fluently; ~150–220 sight words; writes 3–5 sentences with correct punctuation; answers literal and simple inference questions.
**Activities:** digraph/blend **sort and build**, **word-sort by vowel pattern**, **magic-e flip cards**, reading **fluency passages** (repeat 3×), **partner reading**, **story maps**, sentence-building cards, **mini-dictionary**, **spelling games**, **writing journal**.
**Pitfalls:** treating phonics as "done" after CVC; ignoring **tricky/irregular words**; no reading volume; grammar terminology without use; too many rules at once (teach patterns with examples, not rule dumps).

## STAGE 6 — Fluent Reader & Writer (age ~7–8)
**Goal:** reads for **meaning and pleasure** across text types; writes **organised paragraphs** with correct grammar.

### 6.1 Word reading & spelling
| Concept | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Multisyllable words** | Syllable types: closed (rab-bit), open (ba-by), VCe (com-pete), vowel team (rain-bow), r-controlled (car-pet), consonant-le (ta-ble) |
| **Syllable division** | VC/CV (rab-bit), V/CV (ti-ger), VC/V (cab-in), compound words |
| **Prefixes** | un-, re-, dis-, pre-, mis-, in-/im-, non- |
| **Suffixes** | -ful, -less, -ly, -er/-or, -able, -ness, -ment, -ish, -tion/-sion (intro) |
| **Roots/word origins (intro)** | e.g., *port* (carry), *act* |
| **Homophones** | there/their, to/two/too, see/sea, write/right |
| **Homographs & multiple-meaning words** | bat, bark, bow |
| **Irregular spellings** | said, friend, because, though, enough |
| **Spelling rules** | doubling, drop-e, change y→i, plurals; **apostrophes** (contractions, possession) |

### 6.2 Fluency & Comprehension
- **Reading speed benchmark (US norms, approximate; use as a rough guide, esp. for English-as-second-language):** end of Grade 1 (~age 7) ≈ 50–60+ words correct per minute; end of Grade 2 (~age 8) ≈ 90–100+. Indian ESL children often lag the native-speaker norms; track their *own* progress.
- **Comprehension skills:** main idea & key details; sequencing; **cause/effect**; **compare/contrast**; **inference** ("how do you know?"); **predicting**; **summarising**; **vocabulary in context**; **text features** (heading, picture, glossary); **fiction vs non-fiction**; **author's purpose** (inform, entertain, persuade).
- **Text types:** stories, **fables, poems, rhymes**, **informational** text, **instructions**, **letters/notes**, **dialogue**.

### 6.3 Grammar & Language
| Area | Concepts |
|---|---|
| **Parts of speech** | Nouns (common/proper/collective, singular/plural, countable), pronouns, **verbs** (action/helping: is/are/was/were, has/have/had), adjectives (including **comparison**: big/bigger/biggest), **adverbs** (how/when/where), **prepositions**, **conjunctions** (and, but, or, because, so), **articles**, **interjections** |
| **Tenses** | Simple **present**, **past**, **future**; present continuous; irregular verbs (go/went, see/saw) |
| **Sentences** | Statement/question/command/exclamation; **subject + predicate**; **complete vs fragment**; **joining** with and/but/because; **word order**; **agreement** (he runs / they run) |
| **Punctuation & capitalisation** | Full stop, ?, !, commas (lists, dialogue), **apostrophes**, **quotation marks** (intro), **capital letters** (names, places, days, months, titles) |
| **Vocabulary** | **Synonyms/antonyms**, **categories**, **word meanings from context**, **idioms (intro)**, **dictionary skills** (alphabetical order, guide words), **thesaurus** (intro) |
| **Spoken English** | Speaking in **full sentences**, **show-and-tell**, **recitation**, **role-play**, **listening** and answering questions; **Indian-English-aware pronunciation** (see Part 7) |

### 6.4 Writing
- **Paragraph writing:** topic sentence → 3–5 details → closing sentence.
- **Types:** **narrative** (personal story with beginning/middle/end), **descriptive** (my pet, my school), **informative** (simple report), **opinion** (intro: "I like… because…"), **letter/e-mail** (intro), **instructions/recipe**, **poem**.
- **Process (simple):** plan (draw/web) → write → **check** (capitals, full stops, spelling) → improve.
- **Spelling:** weekly words with patterns; **dictation**; **proofreading**.
- **Handwriting:** **cursive** or joined script per school; legible speed; neat spacing; margin and title use.
- **Creative:** story-starters, retell a fable, describe a picture, **diary**, **short poem**.

**Checkpoints (by ~8):** reads grade-level text fluently with expression; decodes multisyllable words; knows ~300+ sight/high-frequency words incl. Dolch Grade 2–3; writes 5–8 sentence paragraph with correct grammar; uses dictionary; summarises a story.
**Activities:** daily independent reading (15–20 min), **read-aloud & discussion**, **book reports**, **word-detective** (prefix/suffix), grammar games (sorting, fill-in, sentence combining), **journal**, **story writing**, **spelling bee**, **library visits**.
**Pitfalls:** grammar through drill-sheets alone; reading only textbooks; writing without feedback; "more worksheets" instead of **more reading**.

# PART 3 — WORD FAMILIES (RIME PATTERNS) LADDER

**Word family = words sharing the same ending ("rime"), differing in the beginning ("onset").** Start only when the child can blend CVC words.

| Level | Families | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Short a (start here)** | **-at, -an, -ap, -ag, -am, -ad, -ab, -ax** | cat/hat/mat; man/can/pan; map/cap/tap; bag/tag/wag; ham/jam/ram; dad/sad/mad |
| **B. Short i** | **-ig, -in, -it, -ip, -im, -id, -ill, -ix** | big/pig/dig; pin/win/fin; sit/hit/bit; lip/sip/tip; him/rim; lid/kid; hill/fill |
| **C. Short o** | **-og, -op, -ot, -ob, -ock, -ox** | dog/log/fog; hop/top/mop; hot/pot/dot; job/rob; sock/clock; box/fox |
| **D. Short e** | **-en, -et, -ed, -eg, -ell, -est** | pen/ten/hen; net/wet/pet; bed/red/fed; leg/peg/beg; bell/sell/well; nest/best |
| **E. Short u** | **-ug, -un, -ut, -ub, -uck, -ump, -unk** | bug/hug/mug; sun/run/fun; cut/nut/hut; cub/rub/tub; duck/truck; jump/bump; junk/skunk |
| **F. Common "-ing / -ang / -ong / -ung / -ink / -ank"** | **-ing, -ang, -ong, -ung, -ink, -ank, -unk** | king/ring/sing; sang/bang; song/long; sung/hung; pink/sink; bank/tank |
| **G. Double-letter families** | **-all, -ell, -ill, -ull, -ess, -iss, -uff** | ball/tall/wall; bell/sell; hill/fill; bull/pull; mess; kiss; puff |
| **H. Long-vowel (silent e) families** | **-ake, -ame, -ate, -ale, -ave, -ike, -ine, -ite, -ide, -ime, -ole, -ose, -one, -ope, -ute, -ube** | cake/bake/lake; name/game/same; gate/late; tale/sale; cave/wave; bike/like; nine/line; kite/bite; ride/hide; time; hole/pole; nose/rose; bone/cone; rope/hope |
| **I. Vowel-team families** | **-ain, -ail, -ay, -eat, -eep, -eed, -ee, -oat, -ow, -ight, -ie, -oon, -ool, -oil** | rain/train; tail/nail; day/play; eat/seat; sleep/deep; seed/feed; tree/free; boat/coat; snow/slow; night/light; pie/tie; moon/spoon; cool/pool; oil/boil |
| **J. R-controlled & other** | **-ar, -ark, -arm, -ore, -orn, -ir, -ur, -air, -ear, -ould, -ound, -ow (cow), -own, -ouse, -ook** | car/star; park/dark; farm/arm; more/store; corn/horn; bird; fur; hair/chair; bear/pear; could/would; round/sound; cow/how; town/down; house/mouse; book/look |

**Teaching sequence for a family:** (1) say the rime, (2) blend onset + rime ("m-at → mat"), (3) read a 6–10-word list, (4) sort pictures/words, (5) write/spell the words, (6) read a 3–5-sentence **decodable passage** containing the family, (7) use in a game; revisit in **mixed review** (so kids read, not memorise).
**Why families help:** pattern spotting accelerates decoding ("if I can read *cat*, I can read *bat*"); **don't** use only families — pair with **blending individual sounds**, otherwise children learn to guess from a chunk.

## 4.2 How to teach sight words (Evidence-informed)
1. **Decodable first.** Many "sight words" are fully decodable (can, it, in, big, run). Teach those by **blending**.
2. **Heart words:** for irregular words (said, was, the, they, one, you, are) teach **the regular parts by sound + the tricky part "by heart"**.
3. **Context & meaning:** use each word in a **spoken sentence**, then a **written sentence**.
4. **Multi-sensory:** say – spell – **build (Velcro/magnets)** – **write** – read in a sentence.
5. **Practice** little and often: **5–10 min** × several times/week; **3–5 new words/week**; always **review old words**.
6. **Read books** rich in those words (decodable + simple stories). **Reading volume** makes words *automatic*.
7. **Not flash-card drill-only**; flash-cards are a **warm-up**, not the lesson.
8. **Phrases:** from S3 onwards, practise **phrases** ("in the box", "I can see") for fluency.

# PART 5 — HANDWRITING / WRITING LADDER (cross-stage)

| Age | Writing milestone | Prerequisite motor | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2–3 | Scribble, vertical/horizontal lines | Gross motor, shoulder stability, whole-hand grip | Big paper, crayons, sand/chalk |
| 3–3½ | Circle, imitate lines; trace simple lines | Digital-pronate/ quadrupod grasp | Fat pencil/crayon, pattern tracing |
| 3½–4½ | Cross, square, diagonal; trace zig-zag/curve; first letters (L I T H E F, O C U S) | Static tripod; scissors snip | Large dotted letters, no lines or wide lines |
| 4½–5½ | **All uppercase** formed; start **lowercase by stroke families**; name writing | Dynamic tripod emerging | Wide-lined (3-line/4-line) books |
| 5½–6½ | **Lowercase** neat; CVC words; **numbers** 0–9; sentence start capital + full stop | Hand dominance set | **Write-wipe boards**, 4-line copy |
| 6½–7½ | Sentences, **spacing**, **ascenders/descenders**, regular size; **dictation** | Fluent hand movement | Single-line / school notebook |
| 7½–8+ | **Cursive/joined** (school policy); paragraphs; speed + neatness | Good endurance | Cursive books |

**Letter-formation tips:** most letters start at the top; the "curly caterpillar" letters (c a d g o q) start with the same anticlockwise curve; use the **same starting point** for each family, **verbal cues** ("down, up, over"), **sky-grass-ground** line names, **trace → copy → independent**.
**Reversal:** b/d, p/q, 6/9, S/Z, N, mirror writing are **normal** to ~7. Persisting beyond with other reading difficulties → screening.

# PART 7 — INDIA-SPECIFIC CONSIDERATIONS

1. **Many Indian children learn English as L2/L3.** NCF-FS wants **oral L2 first**, with **L1 literacy** built concurrently. Strong L1 literacy transfers to English (letter-sound principle, phonological skills).
2. **Letter-name vs sound:** many Indian classrooms teach "A for Apple, B for Ball" with **names**, then struggle with blending. Prefer **sounds + actions** (Jolly Phonics-style) and **pure phoneme pronunciation**.
3. **Phoneme differences between English and Indian languages** (use a native-speaker audio or teacher model; do not rely on printed "pronunciation spelling"):
   - Indian languages are largely **phonetic/syllabic** (akshara); English has **many-to-many sound–spelling** relations → needs more explicit teaching of "tricky words".
   - Common issues: **/v/ vs /w/**, **/θ/ /ð/ (th)**, **schwa** and **unstressed syllables**, **short vowel contrasts** (bit/beat, pen/pan), **word-final consonant clusters**, **/z/ sound**, **stress/intonation**.
4. **Vocabulary gap:** reading comprehension rides on oral vocabulary; **talk, stories, songs, and picture-books in both languages**.
5. **Curriculum pressure:** schools often demand writing/cursive earlier than frameworks recommend. Provide **parent-friendly guidance** that says "pre-writing strokes and finger strength first".
6. **Relevant Indian names/objects:** use *laddoo, diya, rangoli, auto, bus, chappal, tiffin, mango, banana, kite, tabla, cow* to increase relatability in picture vocabulary — but ensure **sound targets** are clear (e.g., 'a' for **a**nt; avoid ambiguous words, e.g., "chappal" for /ch/ is fine).
7. **Multilingual families:** do **not** drop the home language; bilingualism is a strength.

# PART 8 — SCREENING / RED FLAGS & PARENT/TEACHER GUIDANCE (non-diagnostic)

| Age | Consider a pediatrician / speech-language therapist / educational psychologist if… |
|---|---|
| 2 | No 2-word phrases, limited understanding, doesn't point or imitate, loses words |
| 3 | Speech mostly unintelligible to strangers; no sentences; no interest in books/rhymes |
| 4–5 | Can't rhyme or play with sounds; very slow to learn letter names/sounds despite practice; difficulty retelling a story |
| 6–7 | Struggles to blend CVC words, to remember letter-sound links or sight words after months of instruction; **family history of dyslexia**; avoidance of reading; persistent letter reversals with other signs |
| 7–8 | Very slow/inaccurate reading, spelling very poor vs. oral ability, difficulty copying from board |

**Note:** Early difficulty ≠ disability. Check **hearing and vision** first. **Early support** (structured phonics, multisensory) is most effective.
