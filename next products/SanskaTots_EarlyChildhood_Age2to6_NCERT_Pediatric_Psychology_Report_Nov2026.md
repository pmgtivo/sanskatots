# PART 1 — NCERT / NCF SYLLABUS FOR AGE 2–6

## 1.1 Key fact: NCERT has no "syllabus" for age 2–3, and no textbooks for 3–6
| Item | What the framework says |
|---|---|
| Framework | **NCF for Foundational Stage (NCF-FS) 2022**, NCERT, under NEP 2020 |
| Structure | **5+3+3+4**. Foundational Stage = **age 3–8** = 3 yrs pre-school (**Balvatika 1, 2, 3**) + Class 1–2 |
| Age 2 | **Not covered.** Age 0–3 is addressed under the ECCE "care & stimulation" strand (Anganwadi/home). The NCF-FS itself starts at 3 |
| Age 3–6 | **Balvatika** (the "Preparatory" part of the foundational stage). Framework gives **goals + competencies**, not a fixed syllabus or textbook |
| Class 1 entry age | **6 years** |
| Teaching approach | Play-based, activity-based, discovery-based. **No formal reading/writing/arithmetic drills before Class 1** (pre-literacy and pre-numeracy only) |
| Medium | **Mother tongue / home language** preferred through at least Class 5 |
| Learning kit | **Jaadui Pitara** (Magic Box): toys, puzzles, puppets, story cards, flash cards, playbooks, worksheets, in 13 Indian languages. Teacher handbook "Unmukh" |
| Assessment | Observation-based, no exams; "holistic progress card" |

**Implication for you:** Parents of 3–5 yr-olds are often told by private schools to push writing/alphabets. NCERT's own position is the opposite. Your busy-book/tracing/mythology products fit best when positioned as **play-based readiness**, not "early academics". This is a messaging advantage with informed parents.

## 1.4 Things the NCF-FS stresses that most products ignore
1. **Mother tongue first** → vernacular content (Kannada, Hindi, Marathi, Tamil, Telugu, Bengali, Gujarati…) is *policy-aligned*, not just "regional". You already have Kannada; this is a growth lane.
2. **Two languages** (L1 + L2) from the start, oral first.
3. **Seva / productive work** — chores, gardening, helping at home (CG-5). Almost no mainstream kids' product addresses it.
4. **Sensorial sharpness** (CG-2) — sight, sound, touch, smell, taste; Montessori-style.
5. **Nature/environment** (CG-6) — gardening, seasons, animals.
6. **Positive learning habits** (CG-13) — attention, persistence. Busy books and puzzles directly support this.
7. **Panchakosha** (Indian holistic model: physical, vital energy, mental, intellect, bliss) is the NCF's stated philosophical base → a cultural hook for your brand voice.

# PART 2 — PEDIATRICIAN / OT / PUBLIC-HEALTH VIEW (BEYOND SYLLABUS)

## 2.1 Developmental milestones (CDC 2022 revision — "what ≥75% of children do by this age")
Paraphrased from the CDC 2022 checklist; verify exact wording on cdc.gov/act-early/milestones.

| Age | Social / Emotional | Language | Cognitive | Movement / Physical |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2 yr** | Copies others; plays near others; notices others' feelings | ≥2 words together; points to named picture; points to show things | Holds something in one hand while using the other; tries to use switches/buttons; plays with more than one toy | Kicks ball; runs; walks up a few stairs; eats with spoon |
| **2½ yr** | Plays with others, simple pretend | ~50 words; names body parts; follows 2-step instructions | Uses things to pretend; simple problem solving | Jumps off ground with both feet; turns book pages; unscrews lids |
| **3 yr** | Calms within ~10 min after parting; notices and joins other kids | Conversation with ≥2 back-and-forth turns; asks who/what/where/why; strangers understand most speech | Draws a circle; avoids hot objects; strings items (beads); | Puts on some clothes; uses a fork |
| **4 yr** | Pretends to be something else; comforts others; likes to be "helper"; avoids danger | Sentences of ≥4 words; sings parts of songs; talks about their day | Names a few colours; predicts what comes next in a story; draws a person with ≥3 parts | Catches a large ball; serves self food; unbuttons some buttons; holds crayon with fingers |
| **5 yr** | Follows rules/takes turns; does simple chores; sings/dances | Tells story with ≥2 events; keeps conversation going >3 turns; rhymes | Counts to 10; names some numbers 1–5; time words; attention 5–10 min; writes some letters of name; draws person with ≥6 parts | Hops on one foot; buttons some buttons |

## 2.2 Red flags — "see a developmental pediatrician" (CDC/IAP-style; not diagnostic)
| Age | Talk to your doctor if the child… |
|---|---|
| 2 | Doesn't use 2-word phrases; doesn't know what common items are; doesn't copy actions; can't walk steadily; **loses skills** |
| 3 | Doesn't talk in sentences; unclear speech; doesn't play pretend; doesn't make eye contact; doesn't respond to name; extreme difficulty separating; **loses skills** |
| 4 | Can't retell a favourite story; doesn't understand "same/different"; ignores other kids; doesn't respond to people outside family |
| 5 | Doesn't show a wide range of emotions; extremely withdrawn/aggressive; can't focus even briefly; doesn't respond to people; **loses skills** |

**Product relevance:** Never claim a product "treats/cures/fixes speech delay, autism or ADHD". Use "supports", "practice", "fun way to build…". Add a small "Not a substitute for professional assessment" line to parent guides.

## 2.3 Motor skills (OT / pediatric view)

### Gross motor — what pediatricians want
| Guideline | Source | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Total physical activity | WHO 2019 (under-5s) | **≥180 min/day** (age 1–4) of any intensity |
| Energetic play | WHO 2019 | **≥60 min/day** of moderate-to-vigorous (age 3–4) |
| Restrained in stroller/seat | WHO 2019 | **≤1 hour at a time** |
| Sleep | WHO / IAP | 11–14 h (age 1–2); **10–13 h (age 3–5)** incl. naps |
| Skills to practise | OT/physio consensus | running, jumping, hopping, climbing, throwing/catching, balance, kicking, crawling through tunnels, swinging, cycling |

### Fine motor — OT progression
**Pre-writing stroke order (approx. ages when ~most kids copy them):**
scribble (18–24 mo) → vertical line (~2 yr) → horizontal line (~2½) → circle (~3) → cross + (~4) → square (~4½) → diagonals (~5) → triangle & X (~5–6).
*This is the exact order your tracing/pencil-control products should follow.*

**Pencil grasp progression:** fisted (1–2 yr) → digital pronate (2–3) → quadrupod (3–4) → static tripod (3½–4½) → **dynamic tripod (4½–6)**.
*Grasp is built by finger-strength play (clay, tongs, pegs, lacing), not by early writing worksheets.*

**Fine-motor builders (OT-recommended):** playdough, tongs/tweezer transfers, bead stringing/lacing, scissors snipping → cutting, pegs/clothes-pins, buttons/zips/snaps, sticker peeling, tearing paper, threading/sewing cards, building blocks, painting, pouring/scooping, dot-to-dot.
*(Velcro sticker busy books train a pincer pull and bilateral coordination but are low-resistance → pair with a "pegs/pinch" or "lacing" product for real finger-strength work.)*

**Refer to OT if:** can't progress through grasp stages; avoids drawing/cutting; extreme frustration with buttons/zips; hand-dominance still switching after ~6; very weak grip.

### Sensory / sensorial
Touch bins, water/sand play, textures, smell jars, sound shakers, heavy work (carry, push, pull) — all in line with CG-2 and OT "sensory diet".

## 2.4 Screen time (pediatric consensus — directly relevant to your "screen-free" positioning)
| Source | Under 18–24 mo | 2–5 yr |
|---|---|---|
| **IAP (India)** | **No screens** (except occasional video calls) | **≤1 h/day**, supervised, quality content, must not replace play/sleep/interaction |
| **AAP** | Avoid except video chat (<18 mo); co-view quality content (18–24 mo) | ≤1 h/day of high-quality programming, co-view |
| **WHO 2019** | **None** (<2 yr) | **≤1 h/day, less is better**; prefer reading, storytelling, puzzles with a caregiver |

**Voice of Indian developmental pediatrics:** Dr **Samir Dalwai** (New Horizons CDC, Mumbai; contributor to IAP guidance) repeatedly warns that excess screens in the 0–5 window are linked to speech delay, attention issues and behaviour problems, and that **human interaction (talking, storytelling, unstructured play) is the primary intervention**. Your "screen-free" angle has legitimate clinical backing; just don't promise outcomes.

| Domain | Pediatric recommendation (IAP/WHO/AAP) | Product/content angle |

# PART 3 — PSYCHOLOGISTS: WHAT THEY SAY ABOUT HOW KIDS LEARN

## 3.1 Classical developmental theorists
| Thinker | Core idea (age 2–6 relevance) | Product implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Jean Piaget** | Age 2–7 = **preoperational stage**: symbolic thinking, pretend play, egocentric, appearance-based reasoning; struggles with conservation & logic; learns by **doing** | Hands-on, concrete, pretend-play, sorting/sequence; avoid abstract worksheets |
| **Lev Vygotsky** | **Zone of Proximal Development**: kids learn best with *scaffolded* help from adult/peer; language and social interaction drive thinking | Parent-guided activities with "say this / ask this" prompts; "Play-together" formats |
| **Erik Erikson** | Age 1½–3: *Autonomy vs Shame*; **3–6: *Initiative vs Guilt*** — child wants to start things, lead play, make choices | "I can do it myself" products: self-dressing, peel-stick, choose-your-own layouts; praise attempts |
| **Maria Montessori** (physician-educator) | **Sensitive periods** (order, language, movement, sensory, small objects); prepared environment; **practical life**; self-correcting materials | Self-checking activities, real-life skill kits (pouring, buttoning, cutting), minimal clutter |
| **John Bowlby / Mary Ainsworth** | Secure attachment is the base for exploration and learning | Parent-child co-play, bedtime/bonding routines |
| **Albert Bandura** | Kids learn by **observing/modelling** | Parents modelling reading, kindness, chores; reel-friendly "do it together" |
| **Howard Gardner** | Multiple intelligences (popular with parents; weak empirical support as "learning styles") | Fine for variety of activity types; avoid claiming "learning-style" science |

## 3.2 Modern researchers & clinicians
| Researcher | Key finding | Product implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Harvard Center on the Developing Child (Jack Shonkoff)** | **Serve-and-return** interactions build brain architecture; executive function (working memory, flexible thinking, self-control) is the key early-childhood capacity; toxic stress harms it | Co-play products, conversation prompts, routines; "calm" tools |
| **Kathy Hirsh-Pasek / AAP "Power of Play" (Yogman et al., 2018)** | **Guided play** (adult-supported, child-led) beats both passive instruction and unstructured play for learning; the **6 Cs**: Collaboration, Communication, Content, Critical thinking, Creative innovation, Confidence | Frame products around the 6 Cs; "play-with-purpose" |
| **Carol Dweck** | **Growth mindset** — praise **effort/strategy** ("you tried a new way!"), not "you're so smart" | Printed parent tips; "I'm learning!" reward charts; "try again" wipe-clean formats (your write-wipe format *naturally* supports mistake-friendly learning) |
| **Dan Siegel & Tina Payne Bryson** (*The Whole-Brain Child*) | "Name it to tame it"; young brains are emotion-driven; connect before correct | Feelings cards, emotion busy book, calm-down story |
| **Walter Mischel** (marshmallow test) | Self-control matters, but a later replication (Watts, Duncan & Quan, 2018) shows family context explains much of the effect | Waiting/turn-taking games; avoid over-claiming "predicts success" |
| **Alison Gopnik** (*The Gardener and the Carpenter*) | Parents should create a **rich environment** (gardener), not engineer outcomes (carpenter) | Open-ended kits > rigid worksheets; messaging "explore, don't drill" |
| **Adele Diamond** (executive function) | EF is trained by challenging, joyful, socially-embedded play (pretend play, games with rules), not apps | Pretend-play kits, rule-based board/card games for 4–6 |
| **Patricia Kuhl** | Infants/toddlers learn language from **live human interaction**, not from screens | Supports screen-free language products |
| **Diana Baumrind** | **Authoritative** parenting (warm + firm) gives best outcomes | Parent-guide inserts |
| **Peter Gray** | Free play is the child's primary learning mode | Don't over-structure |

## 3.4 Consolidated "what psychologists agree on" (use as brand pillars)
1. **Play is the curriculum** at 2–6 (guided, joyful, hands-on).
2. **Relationships first** — adult interaction is the active ingredient; your products should *invite the parent in*.
3. **Executive function** > early facts. Attention, memory, self-control, flexibility are what school readiness really needs.
4. **Process praise / growth mindset.**
5. **Emotion coaching** (name feelings, then solve).
6. **Autonomy** ("I can do it") at 3–6.
7. **Mother tongue + stories** build identity and language.
8. **Pushing formal academics too early has little long-term benefit** and can reduce motivation (supported by NCF-FS and many ECE bodies).
9. **Screens ≠ interaction.**

# PART 4 — EXTRA-CURRICULAR / ENRICHMENT BY AGE

Rule of thumb from pediatricians/OTs: **1–2 structured activities max under age 6; keep free play ≥ structured time.**

| Area | Age 2–3 | Age 3–4 | Age 4–5 | Age 5–6 | Skills built | Evidence/notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Music (singing, rhythm, simple instruments)** | Action songs, shakers, clapping | Rhyme singing, drum | Keyboard/tabla intro, singing in groups | Formal beginner lessons | Language, memory, rhythm, listening, emotion | Strong support for music play & language; formal instrument training tends to start ~5–6 |
| **Dance** (free, folk, classical basics) | Freestyle | Action rhymes | Folk/Bharatanatyam/Kathak basics | Structured class | Gross motor, balance, coordination, expression, culture | Fits CG-3 & CG-12 |
| **Swimming** | Water play (with parent) | Parent-and-child classes | Lessons | Strokes | Strength, safety, confidence | AAP supports swim lessons from ~1–4 as part of drowning prevention (always supervised) |
| **Yoga / mindful movement** | Animal poses | Story yoga | Yoga + breathing | Yoga | Balance, regulation, calm | Indian cultural fit; use child-yoga, not adult |
| **Sports / outdoor** | Ball, climbing, tricycle | Running games | Football, badminton basics, skipping | Cycling, skating | Gross motor, social, stamina | WHO 180-min target |
| **Art & craft** | Finger paint, crayons | Playdough, collage | Painting, clay, folding | Warli/Madhubani, DIY | Fine motor, creativity, focus | CG-12 |
| **Story-telling / drama / puppetry** | Picture talk | Story time | Role-play, puppet show | Short plays, recitation | Language, empathy, memory | Strong evidence for language/EF |
| **Shloka / bhajan / mantra recitation** | Listening | Short slokas | 4-line slokas | Gita verses, stotras | Memory, phonological awareness, culture | Cultural; language benefits as rhyme/rhythm |
| **Festivals & traditions** | Diya/rangoli with parent | Craft per festival | Role in puja/celebration | Explain "why" | Identity, belonging | Matches *sanskar* brand |
| **Gardening / nature** | Water plants | Seeds | Kitchen garden | Journal | Sensory, patience, CG-6 | Policy aligned |
| **Cooking / kitchen helper** | Washing veg | Stirring, spreading | Simple recipes | Measuring | Maths, motor, independence, seva | Montessori practical life |
| **Building / STEM play** | Blocks | Magnets, Duplo | LEGO, simple circuits (no small parts <3) | Coding-unplugged | Spatial reasoning, problem solving | Block play linked to spatial/maths skills |
| **Board games / chess** | — | Simple turn-taking | Ludo, memory, snakes & ladders | Beginner chess (5–6) | Rules, waiting, planning (EF) | Good EF training; chess benefits for age <6 are modest |
| **Second language / bilingual** | Home language + songs | Add English/Hindi orally | Games/rhymes | Beginning L2 print | CG-9, CG-11 | Bilingualism is healthy |
| **Life-skills / self-help** | Spoon, cup, hand-wash | Dressing | Buttons, serving food | Bag packing, tidy-up | Autonomy, EF | Highest-ROI "activity" |
| **Social playgroup** | 1–2 peers | Small group | Preschool/group | Group | Cooperation, empathy | Needed from ~3 |

### Be sceptical of (weak or no good evidence at age <6)
- **Abacus / "mental maths" classes**, "brain gym", "left-brain/right-brain" programs, "photographic memory" courses, flash-card "genius" programs. Fine as optional fun but don't build claims on them.
- Early formal handwriting worksheets (contradicts NCF-FS and OT guidance).
- Screen-based "educational apps" for under-3s.

# PART 6 — SAFETY, COMPLIANCE & CLAIMS RULES

1. **Small parts & age grading:** Velcro stickers, beads, pegs, pom-poms are choking hazards for children **under 3**. Your current Mythology Busy Book is listed "Age 2+" with 70+ small stickers; many competitors use **3+**. Review the age label and add "adult supervision for under 3" on pack and listing. See your own regulatory section in `SanskaTots_DIYKits_Age2to5_TopIdeas_60DayLaunch_Aug2026.md` (Part 2) for the BIS/toy-safety gate.
2. **Medical claims:** avoid "cures", "treats", "fixes", "diagnoses", "improves IQ", "prevents speech delay/autism". Use "supports", "helps practise", "encourages".
3. **Screen claims:** "Screen-free" is fine; cite "WHO/IAP recommend limiting screens" only with a source link in the parent guide.
4. **Avoid "learning styles" and "left/right brain" claims.**
5. **Milestone charts:** label them "general guide, children develop at different paces; talk to your pediatrician if concerned". Use CDC wording with attribution.
6. **Cultural content:** keep depictions respectful; avoid claiming religious superiority. Fits your sanskar positioning but widens reach if phrased as "culture and values".
7. **Copyright:** don't reproduce NCERT/Jaadui Pitara pages or CDC tables verbatim in sale products; paraphrase and cite.
