## 3. Root-Cause Diagnosis (ranked by sales impact)

### 🔴 ROOT CAUSE #1 — Miscategorized as a Book (highest impact)
Your ASIN is a book (ISBN `8168580842`, `book_display_on_website`, format "Hardcover‑spiral"). Consequences:
- **No "About this item" bullet points.** Amazon's Books layout does not render feature bullets — you lose your single most important conversion + keyword surface.
- **You compete inside the entire Books catalog** (#318,460 rank) instead of the far more relevant *Toys & Games › Learning & Education* nodes where "busy book / quiet book / activity book" buyers actually shop.
- **The recommendation engine associates you with random bestselling books** (Atomic Habits, The Psychology of Laziness) instead of complementary toddler toys, killing cross-sell and relevance signals.
- **Reduced ad efficiency** — Sponsored Products in Books targets book buyers, not toy/gifting buyers.
- **No Toy-specific browse traffic** (e.g., "Toys for 1 year old", "Montessori toys", gift filters).

### 🔴 ROOT CAUSE #2 — No social proof (1 review, 4.0★)
Conversion on toddler/gifting products is review-driven. Competitors have hundreds to thousands of reviews. With 1 review, even buyers who find you don't convert, which further suppresses organic rank (rank ← sales velocity ← conversion).

### 🔴 ROOT CAUSE #3 — Not leveraging Brand Registry / Brand Store
Byline is "by Nagarathna R" (an author), with no "Visit the SanskaTots Store" link and no linked Brand Store. This means you likely aren't getting full Brand Registry benefits: Sponsored Brands, brand-controlled A+, Vine, Store traffic, and brand-defensive protection.

### 🟡 ROOT CAUSE #4 — Title built for keyword-stuffing, not for the algorithm or the shopper
188 characters of pipe‑separated fragments. It repeats "book" 4×, buries the primary keyword, and omits several high-volume search phrases (see §4). Pipes and duplication waste indexable space.

### 🟡 ROOT CAUSE #5 — Weak conversion assets
No video; images likely lack a benefit-led hero, infographic, "what's inside," size/scale, and lifestyle shots. Reading age "1–10 years" is too broad and reads as unfocused for a toddler product.
